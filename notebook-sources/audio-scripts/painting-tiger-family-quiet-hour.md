## English narration

Some paintings capture what they see. This one captures what they feel — the moment when the light changes, when the activity slows, when the world holds its breath. That's the hour this tiger family lives in.

Most tiger paintings go for power and aggression — bared teeth, hunting poses, dominance. But that was never the whole story of these animals, and it wasn't the story I wanted to tell. The problem was how to avoid the cliché while still capturing what a tiger actually is: not just a hunter, but a parent.

The turn came through the eyes. I directed the mother's gaze away from any confrontation. She isn't looking at you, and she isn't looking at her cubs — she's looking into the distance. That single choice is the whole painting: the watchfulness of protection, constant but calm. It took several studies of different eye directions to land there, but the calm vigilance was worth the search.

Here's something most people miss. Look at where the mother is standing — between you and her cubs. Not aggressive, not threatening. Just there. And the cub, half-hidden behind her, leaning into that shelter while its curiosity pulls it forward. That tension between safety and exploration is the quietest, truest thing in the frame.

This painting was built to catch warm evening light. The golds and oranges glow during the hour before sunset; by seven the shadows deepen and a protective atmosphere takes over. I imagine it in a west-facing living room, a quiet study, a reading nook — somewhere evening light reaches the canvas for an hour each day. Whoever reads there will look up in their own quiet hour and find a kind of strength in this family's still presence.

So come and see it for yourself. Stand in front of it in the late light, and if it speaks to you, message me on WhatsApp — I'd love to tell you more about how it was made.

## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how this painting was made" handout deck for the gallery painting **"Cranes at Sunset — The Hour the Cranes Cross"** (acrylic, 2 × 1.5 ft). Use only the supplied source. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "Cranes at Sunset · The Hour the Cranes Cross," Acrylic 2 × 1.5 ft, gallery piece, one-line hook: *a moment, not a subject — the few seconds at dusk when cranes cross the last light and the water goes still.*
2. **The stillness problem** — birds in flight are usually painted as action (wings spread, motion blurred); the brief wanted the opposite — calm, near-suspended, the quiet before they land; the buyer wanted a painting that lowers the room's pulse, not raises it.
3. **The turn — one wash, never touched** — the water laid in a single wet wash and left entirely alone; the cranes float on stillness rather than standing in it; early versions added ripples and reflections that made the scene busier; removing them was the real work.
4. **No hard edge anywhere** — the discipline of the piece: not a single crisp line to land the eye on; the cranes are soft graphite, kept nearly monochrome, so they read as silhouette, not specimen.
5. **The last ten minutes of light** — Amber sky (the exact amber of the ten minutes before sunset, not a generic orange), Pale rose water (the sky's warmth cooled and flattened into the wash), Soft graphite cranes (kept monochrome so they read as silhouette).
6. **How it behaves in a room** — Midday reads as flat warm light; 6 PM the sky and water meet and the cranes lift off the surface; Dusk the whole piece dims to a single glowing band; most alive at the hour it depicts.
7. **Where it belongs** — end of a corridor, above a bath, a west-facing wall; for the stillness seeker who values calm, likes negative space, wants art that lowers the pulse.
8. **Closing slide** — studio line: *every painting eventually leaves the studio; the story it carries never does* — and a pointer to message on WhatsApp.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

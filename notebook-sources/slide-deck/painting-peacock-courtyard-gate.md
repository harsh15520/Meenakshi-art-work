## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how this gate was made" handout deck for the commissioned gate **"Peacock Courtyard Gate — The Gate That Says Welcome"** (carved wood, 6 × 4 ft, for a private courtyard entrance). Use only the supplied source. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "Peacock Courtyard Gate · The Gate That Says Welcome," Carved Wood 6 × 4 ft, commissioned entrance, one-line hook: *the first object every guest meets, built to say "welcome" the way a family actually means it.*
2. **The family's request** — a carved wooden gate for a private courtyard entrance, hand-painted with peacocks beneath a flowering tree, to greet guests before they step inside; the client's own words about saying welcome with live birdsong and flowers.
3. **The wedding-invitation peacocks** — the birds echo the couple's wedding invitation, a private joke and love story carved at the threshold; "A marriage, remembered at the threshold."
4. **The craft problem** — a carved gate and a painted gate are two different crafts; let carving do structure and paint do life without fighting; the clients wanted one object made by one hand, not a carved frame with a picture.
5. **The material choice** — carved seasoned wood, sealed, with layered acrylic on the relief; wood gives weight and shadow, acrylic holds bright peacock colour without cracking outdoors; alternatives rejected: pure relief (lost colour), printed panel (defeated handmade), metal inlay (too cold).
6. **The 5-week timeline** — Week 1 carve arch + neem trunk → Weeks 2–3 peacocks in relief carved then underpainted → Week 4 blossoms + sealing → Week 5 installation in Saharanpur, checked in morning light.
7. **Studio notes** — make the neem the frame not the backdrop; peacock eyes in slight relief catch the sun, flat paint alone looked printed.
8. **Two details people miss** — relief peacock eyes carved a few mm proud catch sunlight (most notice the shimmer, not the reason); the neem is the frame, its branches form the arch — step back and the gate is the tree.
9. **Installation & living now** — fitted to the courtyard entrance, the family stood back as morning sun hit the carved peacocks and the relief read alive; guests pause and photograph the gate before the house; the neem leaves shift through the day; it became the family's "we're home" marker.
10. **Closing slide** — studio line: *some commissions are about a thing; this one was about an entrance* — and a pointer to message on WhatsApp for commissions.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

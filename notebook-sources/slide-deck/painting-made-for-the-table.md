## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how these plates were made" handout deck for the commissioned table set **"Made for the Table — Flowers She Can Eat Off"** (hand-painted wood; Jasmine · Wood Plate, round, 16 in; plates and a tray sent from a daughter to her mother). Use only the supplied source. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "Made for the Table · Flowers She Can Eat Off," hand-painted wood, round 16 in, a commissioned birthday gift, one-line hook: *made to be used and washed, not stored and admired.*
2. **The daughter's request** — a set of hand-painted wooden plates and a tray with jasmine and magnolia, sent to her mother for her birthday — beautiful, but safe to actually eat from; the client's own words: *"She feeds us all on them now — the flowers match the jasmine in her garden."*
3. **Two client notes that shaped it** — (a) could the jasmine match the ones in her mother's garden, not a generic flower, *her* jasmine; (b) make the tray safe for actual food, not just display — she'll use it, not store it.
4. **The problem: beautiful and usable** — painted tableware usually fails one way, too precious to use or too plain to love; this brief demanded both; the mother would use them daily, so a display-only gift would have been a quiet insult.
5. **The material: sealed wood** — acrylic-painted wood finished with a food-safe sealant; wood takes floral colour warmly and, once sealed, survives washing and daily food; rejected — ceramic (client wanted the lighter wood feel), untreated wood (not food-safe), printed melamine (no hand behind it).
6. **Her garden, exactly (palette)** — white jasmine matched to the mother's actual garden, not a generic bloom; soft magnolia pink on the companion plate, a second note so the set reads as a pair not a repeat; leaf green scrollwork carrying the eye around the rim so the flower sits in a garden, not floating.
7. **Four weeks to the birthday** — Week 1 wood prep and base (sealed and primed) → Weeks 2–3 florals, jasmine and magnolia painted by hand on each piece → Week 4 food-safe top coat, dispatched to Pune for the birthday.
8. **The notebook + details people almost miss** — Day 3, match the jasmine to her garden photo, not a stock flower; Day 18, three coats of sealant, it has to survive a dishwasher not just a shelf; the garden-matched jasmine as the daughter's one non-negotiable, and the invisible sealant coats as the reason the art survives the sink.
9. **Living on the table + closing slide** — the mother serves the family on them daily, exactly as asked; the jasmine is recognised as "hers" by visiting grandchildren; the tray became the birthday-table centre piece, then stayed. Close on the studio line: *every painting eventually leaves the studio; the story it carries never does* — and a pointer to message on WhatsApp for a table set.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

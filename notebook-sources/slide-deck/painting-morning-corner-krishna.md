## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how this painting was made" handout deck for the commissioned painting **"The Morning Corner — The First Face of the Morning"** (acrylic on canvas, 2 × 3 ft, for a home prayer corner). Use only the supplied source. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "The Morning Corner · The First Face of the Morning," Acrylic 2 × 3 ft, commission for the Sharma Family, one-line hook: *a corner where gold catches the first ray of day.*
2. **The grandmother's tradition** — the Sharma family came with a tradition, not just a request: their grandmother always said Krishna should be the first face seen each morning; this painting honors that. (No separate testimonial exists; the client voice is the client-memory module.)
3. **The reference choice** — built from the family's own temple photograph, a living deity they grew up with; a generic stock temple reference was rejected for lacking the family's personal thread.
4. **The morning light (6 / 9 / 12)** — 6 AM first ray lands on the gold crown as a warm focal point; 9 AM east windows fill the room and the whole painting glows; 12 PM overhead light reveals the depth of a 15-layer background.
5. **Two changes to the face** — Day 3, Krishna's eyes set at prayer-mat height so the figure meets you where you kneel; Day 12, gold pigment test #3 with warm undertones finally reads true in morning light.
6. **The details most miss** — Krishna's eyes never look directly at you (gently lowered, reverent, avoiding confrontation); and a lotus at his feet added though not in the reference, for the grandmother who loved lotuses — her presence in the prayer.
7. **6 AM installation** — delivered and installed at 6 AM so the client saw it in the exact light it was painted for; they watched the first light hit the gold crown as intended.
8. **Living in the corner** — prayer mat moved three times to catch the 6:15 gold; fresh marigolds every Friday; the mother prays there, reminded of her childhood temple; three weeks later the lamp moved opposite for better evening glow; two months later the daughter lights the diya — now her morning responsibility.
9. **Closing slide** — studio line: *every painting eventually leaves the studio; the story it carries never does* — and a pointer to message on WhatsApp for a commission.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

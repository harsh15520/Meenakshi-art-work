## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how this painting was made" handout deck for the gallery painting **"Graphite Portrait — A Face in Pencil Only"** (graphite on cold-press paper, 12 × 16 in). Use only the supplied source. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "Graphite Portrait · A Face in Pencil Only," Graphite 12 × 16 in, gallery piece, one-line hook: *a single grey field, graded by how hard the pencil bore down.*
2. **The colour problem** — a graphite portrait risks reading like a study, not a finished piece, as if the colour "hasn't been added yet"; the buyer wanted character without decoration — a face, held, nothing else.
3. **The turn — pressure as pigment** — tone built from how hard the pencil pressed, not from more marks; one gradient from the dark jawline to the pale chin carries the whole face; layering made it muddy until a single pressure-gradient cleared it.
4. **Three grades, one hand** — graphite 2B through 6B on cold-press paper; soft B grades give the full pressure range, paper tooth holds the lightest grey without shine; why not charcoal (too smudgy), coloured pencil (defeats monochrome), digital (loses the pressure).
5. **Two quiet techniques** — lifted-pressure hatching (strokes begin dark, rise off the paper, line fades without a hard stop) and negative drawing for highlights (lightest areas left as bare paper, stay cool while shadows warm).
6. **The detail most miss** — the whole likeness lives in one dark-to-pale jawline; the brightest spots are bare paper never drawn, which is why they stay cool.
7. **Closing slide** — for someone who reads a drawing as drawing, notices craft, likes restraint, wants a study that is also finished — and a pointer to message on WhatsApp.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

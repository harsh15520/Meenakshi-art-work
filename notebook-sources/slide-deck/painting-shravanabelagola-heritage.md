## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how this painting was made" handout deck for the commissioned painting **"Shravanabelagola · Ruins & Monolith"** (acrylic, 2 × 3 ft, painted for a son, for his father). Use only the supplied source. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "Shravanabelagola · Ruins & Monolith," Acrylic 2 × 3 ft, a heritage commission for a father, one-line hook: *a hill painted small, carried into a study.*
2. **The son's request** — a painting of Shravanabelagola's ruins and monolith, not a postcard but the hill as the father remembers it, to hang above his bookshelf; the client's own words about bringing the hill back into his study.
3. **Memory, not monument** — the monolith asked for "small, like a memory," the opposite of a hero-shot; the challenge was to make a famous landmark feel intimate and remembered.
4. **Why this hill** — the monolithic statue of Bahubali is one of the largest free-standing statues in the world, but the client wanted it as the small, familiar thing his father climbed toward; plus a personal landmark, the step where the father rested.
5. **The composition turn** — the hero composition was rejected (too postcard, the monolith dominated and the memory disappeared); the intimate composition was chosen, with the monolith small among the ruins as the father would have seen it climbing.
6. **The palette** — warm stone grey for the ruins (the granite at golden hour, not cold grey); dusty green hillside to keep the scene grounded; pale sky left thin so the eye rests on the small monolith.
7. **The climb light** — Dawn (the monolith a silhouette against first light), Midday (flat and honest, the climb as remembered), Dusk (the stone warms, the memory closest) — the light-simulator spine.
8. **Details people almost miss** — the small monolith painted intentionally small; the resting step, a personal landmark among the famous ones.
9. **Living in the study** — hung above the bookshelf where the father reads; family conversations about the boyhood climbs start with "look at the painting"; the small monolith reads larger the longer you live with it.
10. **Closing slide** — studio line about a heritage commission carrying a family's memory into a room, and a pointer to message on WhatsApp.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how this painting was made" handout deck for the gallery painting **"Village Well · Banyan — The Well Everyone Knew"** (acrylic on canvas, 2 × 3 ft). Use only the supplied source. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "Village Well · Banyan · The Well Everyone Knew," Acrylic 2 × 3 ft, gallery piece, one-line hook: *about a routine, not a place — the walk to the well, the wait in the banyan's shade.*
2. **The postcard problem** — village scenes slide into bright flat 'colourful India'; the danger was a pretty postcard instead of a remembered place; the buyer grew up at such a well and wanted its weight, not the brochure.
3. **The turn — painting the tree in threads** — banyan aerial roots laid with a nearly-empty brush, hundreds of separate dry strokes; solid and whole from far, fragile threads up close.
4. **Earth, not rainbow** — terracotta earth (the actual village soil, ground and pots); dusty olive (the banyan, muted and old, not decorative); pale sky wash left thin so the eye rests.
5. **Why the banyan, why the well** — the banyan as the village meeting place and institution where news is settled; the well as the day's first errand and the village's original social network.
6. **The details people miss** — dry-brush roots dissolve into individual strokes close, whole again from across the room; the chipped water pot, second from left, kept uncorrected on purpose.
7. **Living with it** — a dining-room conversation piece visitors from villages recognise as their own well; earth tones pair with wood and terracotta; one owner said it "smells like childhood."
8. **Where it belongs** — a home far from the village, a small daily reminder in soft morning light: dining room, stair landing, study.
9. **Closing slide** — studio line: *every painting eventually leaves the studio; the story it carries never does* — and a pointer to switch on the page's ambient sound (pulley, wind, sparrows) and message on WhatsApp.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how this portrait was made" handout deck for the commissioned **"Occasion Portrait — The Way She Looked That Day"** (graphite, 12 × 16 in, for a daughter's wedding). Use only the supplied sources. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "Occasion Portrait · The Way She Looked That Day," Graphite 12 × 16 in, commissioned by a mother for a daughter's wedding, one-line hook: *made to capture a day, not just a person.*
2. **A mother's commission** — styled graphite portraits for a daughter's wedding, capturing the jewellery, the embroidery and the way her hair was pinned so the look of that day could be kept; the client's own words: *"She wanted portraits that felt like the way she looked that day."*
3. **Why the wedding** — a daughter's wedding; not a generic pretty face but a specific, once-in-a-life look the mother wanted preserved exactly.
4. **Drawing the details, not just the face** — a wedding portrait fails if the jhumkas, embroidery and hairpin disappear into "suggested" detail; the challenge was to render the occasion, not just the person; those details were the whole point.
5. **Two things the client added** — (a) the sangeet jhumkas, drawn as a named detail rather than a generic stud, so the portrait names the specific celebration; (b) the venue light, shifting the ground to a warm wash so the portrait carries the feeling of the room, not a studio grey.
6. **Graphite, with a little warmth** — graphite on warm-toned paper, embroidery picked out in fine white; rejected: full colour (too photo-like), pure charcoal (lost the fine jewellery), digital (no hand behind it).
7. **Three weeks to the day** — Week 1 reference & likeness → Week 2 jewellery & embroidery → Week 3 background & finishing, delivered before the memories faded.
8. **Studio notes** — Day 4: draw the jhumka first, it sets the whole festive read; Day 12: warm wash, not white ground, or it looks like a passport photo.
9. **The four-colour palette** — Gold Jewellery `#c9a227` (festive anchor), Rose Embroidery `#b86b7a` (wedding palette), Warm Paper `#f3e6d2` (intimate, not clinical), Emerald Accent `#2f6b4f` (one cool note, so the warm reads as celebration).
10. **Details people almost miss** — the named jhumkas (the client's real request, preserved); the embroidery thread followed stitch by stitch, clearly hand-drawn, not printed.
11. **Closing slide** — studio line: *every painting eventually leaves the studio; the story it carries never does* — and a pointer to message on WhatsApp for occasion portraits.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

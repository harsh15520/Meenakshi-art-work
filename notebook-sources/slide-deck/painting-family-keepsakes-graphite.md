## Describe the slide deck you want to create

Create a short, detailed "how these portraits were made" handout deck for the commissioned family set **"Five Faces We Come From"** (graphite, 12 × 16 in, a set of five, for a home hallway). Use only the supplied source. Build slides in this order:

1. **Title slide** — "Five Faces We Come From," Graphite 12 × 16 in (set of five), commissioned family gift, one-line hook: *drawn so a hallway could hold the people a family comes from.*
2. **The daughter's request** — five graphite portraits from family photographs — the parents, the husband's parents, and the grandmother who raised her — to hang together in a hallway; the client's own words about remembering who she comes from.
3. **Why it mattered** — the set wasn't decorative; it was a way to keep the grandmother who raised her, and both sets of parents, present in daily life; grandmother, parents, and the couple who commissioned them, all on one wall.
4. **The temperature problem** — five photographs from different decades, lighting and quality all over the place; the risk of five portraits that looked like five different artists; they had to read as one family, one wall, not a collage of strangers.
5. **Two decisions that held it together** — (a) the Diwali photographs won — everyone looked happiest and most themselves; the formal studio shots rejected, too stiff, reading as ID photos; (b) the soft living-room light — soft graphite as one material, bare-paper cool highlights, a single warm mat board tying five faces into one wall.
6. **The notebook** — Day 2, draw the grandmother first, she's the anchor the others hang around; Day 9, keep every face soft, like the client's living-room light, not a sharp studio.
7. **The detail most people miss** — all five drawn with the same hand-pressure range so they read as one set from across the hall; the grandmother drawn first and centred, the quiet hierarchy of the wall.
8. **Living in the hallway** — hung at eye level so the family is met, not displayed; visitors name the faces, it reads as a family not a gallery; the grandmother's portrait became the one guests ask about first.
9. **Closing slide** — studio line: *every painting eventually leaves the studio; the story it carries never does* — and a pointer to message on WhatsApp for a family set.

Keep all text short and phrase-level. English.

## Painting summary

Village Well · Banyan is a painting about a routine, not a place — the walk to the well, the wait in the banyan's shade, the same task done by the same hands for generations. It refuses the postcard and keeps the weight of a remembered village.

## The postcard problem

Village scenes slide easily into postcards: bright, flat, that cheerful 'colourful India.' The danger was making something pretty instead of something remembered. The person who asked for this grew up at such a well — what they wanted wasn't a brochure, it was the weight of the place.

## The turn — painting the tree in threads

A banyan's aerial roots are the hardest thing to paint honestly; as a solid green mass they look like a cartoon. They were painted with a nearly-empty brush, hundreds of separate dry strokes, each root laid down one at a time. From far the tree reads solid and whole; step close and you see it is only threads holding together — how real banyan roots actually hang.

## Earth, not rainbow

- **Terracotta earth** — the colour of the actual village soil; the ground and the pots.
- **Dusty olive** — a muted green that lets the banyan feel old, not decorative.
- **Pale sky wash** — left thin on purpose so the eye can rest.

## Why the banyan, why the well

The banyan is the village meeting place and institution — where news, matches and arguments are settled. The well is the day's first errand, the village's original social network; painting it is painting that rhythm.

## The details people miss

Step close to the roots and the solid tree dissolves into hundreds of individual dry strokes; from across the room it is whole again. The second pot from the left keeps its real chip — left uncorrected on purpose, because a memory isn't tidy.

## Living with it

In a dining room it becomes a conversation piece: visitors from villages recognise their own well instantly. The earth tones sit with wood and terracotta; one owner said it "smells like childhood." It belongs in a home far from the village, a small daily reminder in soft morning light — a dining room, a stair landing, a study.

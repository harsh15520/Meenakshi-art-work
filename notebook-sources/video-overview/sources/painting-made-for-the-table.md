## Set summary

Made for the Table is a commissioned set of hand-painted wooden plates and a tray — the Jasmine plate is round, sixteen inches, acrylic on wood under a food-safe finish. Some commissions are for the wall. This one was for the table: sent from a daughter to her mother, meant to be used and washed, not stored and admired.

## The brief

A set of hand-painted wooden plates and a tray with jasmine and magnolia, sent to the client's mother for her birthday — beautiful, but safe to actually eat from. In the client's words: *"She feeds us all on them now — the flowers match the jasmine in her garden."* The testimonial that opened the commission: *"I sent these to my mother for her birthday. She feeds us all on them now — the flowers match the jasmine in her garden."* Gift from a daughter; four weeks; delivered to Pune.

## Two client notes that shaped it

- **Her jasmine, not a jasmine** — *"Could the jasmine match the ones in my mother's garden — not a generic flower, her jasmine?"*
- **Food-safe, not display-safe** — *"Please make the tray safe for actual food, not just display — she'll use it, not store it."*

## The problem

Painted tableware usually fails one way: too precious to use, or too plain to love. This brief demanded both — food-safe and genuinely beautiful. The mother was going to use them daily, so a display-only gift would have been a quiet insult.

## The material

Acrylic-painted wood, finished with a food-safe sealant. Wood takes the floral colour warmly and, once sealed, survives washing and daily food. Ceramic was considered but the client wanted the lighter wood feel; untreated wood is not food-safe; printed melamine has no hand behind it.

## Her garden, exactly

- **White jasmine** — the daughter's request, matched to the mother's actual garden, not a generic bloom.
- **Soft magnolia pink** — the companion plate, a second note so the set reads as a pair, not a repeat.
- **Leaf green scrollwork** — the join, carrying the eye around the rim so the flower sits in a garden, not floats.

## The four-week build

Week 1 was wood prep and base — plates and tray sealed and primed for paint. Weeks 2–3 were the florals, jasmine and magnolia painted by hand on each piece. Week 4 brought sealing and dispatch: the food-safe top coat applied, the set sent to Pune for the birthday. The notebook carries both halves — Day 3, *match the jasmine to her garden photo, not a stock flower*; Day 18, *three coats of sealant — it has to survive a dishwasher, not just a shelf*.

## Details people almost miss

- **The garden-matched jasmine** — painted from the mother's actual garden photo, the daughter's one non-negotiable.
- **The sealant coats** — three coats of food-safe finish; invisible, but the reason the art survives the sink.

## Living on the table

The mother serves the family on them daily — exactly as asked. The jasmine on the plate is now recognised as "hers" by visiting grandchildren. The tray became the birthday-table centre piece, then stayed.

## Visual cues for the video

- Open on the sunlit table with the plates and tray in use, then move in close on the hand-painted florals — the visual star is the hand: individual petals, the leaf-green scrollwork tracing the rim.
- Slow trace of the scrollwork all the way around the rim, so the eye completes the circle and the bloom reads as sitting in a garden.
- The invisible star: show the food-safe sealant as three passes of clear coat over a finished petal — the coats never change the colour, then water and washing pass over the same petal and the flower is still there.
- Compress the four-week build: bare wood prep → priming → jasmine and magnolia painted by hand → the clear top coat → the packed set leaving for Pune.
- Cut between the mother's garden jasmine and the painted jasmine to show the match that was the daughter's non-negotiable.
- Close on the tray as the centre of a laid table, plates in daily use, and the studio line: *every painting eventually leaves the studio; the story it carries never does.*

## How it connects

This is the anchor of the custom-orders "Made for the Table" room — proof that a commission does not have to hang on a wall to carry a family's story; it can be handed over, eaten off, washed, and used every day.

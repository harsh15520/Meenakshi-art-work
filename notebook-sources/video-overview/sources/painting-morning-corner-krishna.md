## Painting summary

The Morning Corner is a home commission — acrylic, two by three feet — painted so that a family's day opens on Krishna's face. It isn't a generic deity: it's the Sharma family's own tradition made visible, sourced from the single temple photograph they grew up with.

## The grandmother's tradition

The Sharma family came with a tradition, not just a request: their grandmother always said Krishna should be the first face you see in the morning. There's no separate client testimonial for this piece — the client's voice *is* the grandmother's words, carried by the family into the brief.

## The reference

The painting was built from one photograph: the family's own temple image, the deity they grew up with. That's the entire reference story as it exists today — a single, personal source, not a shortlist that got narrowed down.

## The turn (two changes made mid-painting)

On day three the client asked that Krishna's eyes sit at prayer-mat height, so the figure meets the family where they kneel, not from above. On day twelve the gold pigment finally behaved — test number three, with warm undertones, the one that reads true in morning light. Both changes moved the painting from a picture of Krishna toward this family's Krishna specifically.

## Four details worth lingering on

- **The peacock crown** — worked with tiny peacock plumes, the artist's own signature motif, positioned to catch the first ray of morning light before anything else in the room.
- **The floral offerings** — marigold and lotus garlands at the base, painted from the actual flowers the family places there each week.
- **The flute and the Om mark** — one hand holds the flute, the other the quiet Om: two symbols of the sound that opens the day in this home.
- **The gaze** — Krishna's eyes are set at prayer-mat height, so the first face a child sees at dawn is the one their grandmother asked for.

## The 6 AM installation and what came after

The painting was delivered and installed at 6 AM, so the family could see it in the exact light it was painted for — they watched together as the first morning light struck the gold crown as intended. Since then: the prayer mat was moved three times before they found the spot where light hits the gold at 6:15 AM; fresh marigolds now arrive every Friday, part of the weekly ritual; the client's mother visits specifically to pray in this corner, saying the gold reminds her of the temple she grew up visiting. Three weeks in, the family moved a lamp to the opposite wall because evening light made the painting glow better than the electric lamp did. Two months in, the daughter learned to light the diya herself — now her morning responsibility.

## Where it belongs

A home prayer corner that gets direct morning light — this piece was built for one specific corner in one specific home, not for a generic setting.

## Painting summary

Radha-Krishna in Gold & Blue is painted not as fire but as dusk — blue settling, gold answering, two figures who don't need to face each other to be together. It is the collection's quiet sacred piece, and its making turns on one technique: gold laid first, then luminous blue floated wet-on-wet over its edge.

## The temperature call

Three references were studied. A moonlit version was cool and beautiful but lost the earthly warmth the buyer wanted. A theatre duet was lively but too loose for the centre wall. The chosen midday study is a warm blue that stays luminous rather than dark — keep the night warm so devotion can still be felt in it.

## The turn — gold first, blue second (the visual star)

The real move is at the easel: gold first, blue second, or the halo dies. The warm gold halo is laid first, then the luminous blue is floated wet-on-wet over its edge so the two breathe into each other. The blue is mixed to glow, not to recede — the hardest pigment to keep alive on a dark field — and laying the gold first is exactly what lets it live. A touch of rose on Radha keeps her human amid the divine blue; on the fifth pass her rose was pulled warmer because she had started reading as another deity, not a woman.

## Where the gold catches

- **Morning, east window** — the halo reads as pale fire; the blue stays recessive.
- **Noon, flat light** — gold and blue reach equal weight, the most balanced read.
- **Evening, lamp** — the halo glows and the blue deepens into night.

## The detail most miss

The flute lies at the side, resting, not raised — the music is implied, the moment is the pause between notes. Their hands: the fingers almost meet but don't — the tension of restrained devotion, not union. Stand close and you feel it more than you read it.

## Where it belongs

Above a console where the evening lamp is lit before guests arrive, the gold catching them at the door — a pooja-room antechamber, a dining console, or a stair landing. It is for someone who doesn't need the figures named: who feels the blue-and-gold temperature and already knows the love it points to, who likes warmth over spectacle and wants a daily, not a display.

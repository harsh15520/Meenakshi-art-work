## Painting summary

Lion · Pop Art is the collection's deliberate break from tradition — flat colour, hard edges, a stare borrowed from print rather than the wild. It doesn't observe a lion; it declares one.

## The realism trap

A lion almost has to be painted realistic: mane, fur, savannah, the whole nature documentary. This brief wanted to break that reflex and treat the lion as a graphic object, not a nature study. The buyer wanted range on the wall — a loud counterpoint to the rest of the collection.

## The turn — repetition as the mane

The mane wasn't drawn stroke by stroke. It was built from repeated stencil-like shapes, so the pattern itself becomes the subject and the lion becomes a poster. Free-drawn manes kept drifting back toward realism no matter how many attempts; only when a fixed, repeated unit was locked in did the graphic read finally hold.

## Four colours, no apology

- **Signal orange** — the body, the first colour the eye lands on.
- **Cobalt blue** — the negative space, pushing the orange forward and keeping the poster feel.
- **Hot magenta** — mane accents, the third pop so it never settles into two tones.
- **Black outline** — the hold; hard edges, never painterly.

## Keeping it flat

Each colour was blocked in clean with masked flat fills — no blending across boundaries, because blending would betray the print language. Every edge got the same black line at the same weight; that equal weight is what makes it read as a poster and not a sketch.

## Where it belongs

A young person's room, a studio corner, an entrance that should pop — anywhere energy is wanted more than calm. For someone who wants the wall to speak first: bold, graphic, unapologetic.

## Gate summary

Peacock Courtyard Gate is a welcome gate — six by four feet of carved, sealed wood with layered acrylic on the relief — made for a private courtyard entrance. It isn't a picture hung on a wall; it's the first object every guest meets, built to say "welcome" the way a family actually means it.

## The brief

The family asked for a carved wooden gate for a private courtyard entrance, hand-painted with peacocks beneath a flowering tree, to greet guests before they stepped inside. In their words: something that would say welcome *"the way our family actually welcomes — with live birdsong and flowers."* The testimonial that opened the commission: *"This gate is the first thing guests see. It was meant to say 'welcome' the way our family actually welcomes — with live birdsong and flowers."* The peacocks echo the couple's wedding invitation — a private joke and a love story carved at the threshold.

## The craft problem

A carved gate and a painted gate are two different crafts. The challenge was to let the carving do the structure and the paint do the life, without one fighting the other. The clients wanted one object made by one hand — not a carved frame with a picture stuck on.

## The material

Carved seasoned wood, sealed, with layered acrylic on the relief. Wood gives the gate its weight and shadow; acrylic holds the bright peacock colour without cracking on an outdoor-facing surface. Set aside: pure relief carving (lost the colour story), a printed panel (defeated the handmade point), metal inlay (too cold for a home entrance).

## The 5-week build

Week 1 carved the arch and the neem trunk — the wood shaped and the tree's form roughed in before any colour. Weeks 2–3 carved each peacock in relief, then underpainted it so the feather relief caught light. Week 4 filled in the flowering tree and sealed the whole gate against weather. Week 5 brought installation in Saharanpur, fitted to the courtyard entrance and checked in the morning light.

## The two details

- The relief peacock eyes are carved a few millimetres proud so they catch sunlight — most visitors notice the shimmer, not the reason.
- The neem is the frame: its branches form the arch's outline. Step back and the gate is the tree.

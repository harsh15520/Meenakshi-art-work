## Mural summary

The Churning of the Ocean is a farewell mural — eight by six feet of acrylic on an institutional wall — made to honor a mentor who gave everything. It isn't a portrait of a person; it's mythology that speaks about effort itself, so that future generations walking past will see what persistence looks like.

## The brief

The institution asked for something monumental — not just the achievement, but the effort. In their words: a farewell gift that would *"remind future generations of what persistence looks like."* The testimonial that opened the commission: *"It had to feel monumental — it had to hold the whole story."*

## The scale challenge

What reads on canvas doesn't always survive a wall. The figures had to be legible from across a large hall and still hold detail up close. The method was patient: multiple scale studies, then viewing-angle tests from many points in the hall, before any colour was committed.

## Two requests that shaped it

- **Vasuki wraps the whole composition** — the serpent is the backbone of the story, not a side detail.
- **Treasures emerge gradually** — achievement as a process, not a moment; some treasures fully formed, others still becoming.

## The 8-week build

Week 1 aligned on philosophy — why the churning metaphor mattered. Week 2 prepared and tested the institutional wall surface in sections. Weeks 3–4 resolved scale and figure composition for balance from every angle. Weeks 5–6 layered colour in mural acrylics built to withstand institutional lighting. Week 7 refined Vasuki and the emerging treasures. Week 8 brought installation, the final touches laid on the day of the farewell ceremony as students and faculty watched.

## The transformation

A blank, functional dividing wall became a teacher. Students pause on their way to class and some start researching the story; faculty use it to discuss perseverance and collective effort; it is now the landmark everyone gives directions from.

## Painting summary

Tiger Family is where the studio chose quiet strength over the usual roar. Instead of power and aggression — bared teeth, hunting poses — the painting shows protection, family bonds, and the watchfulness of a mother who is not looking at you at all, but into the distance. It lives in the hour before sunset, and it was built to glow in that exact light.

## The problem it avoids

Tiger paintings almost always reach for dominance. The goal here was the opposite: a family, not a threat. The challenge was to avoid the cliché while keeping the animal unmistakably a tiger.

## The turn — the gaze

The resolution was in the eyes. The mother's gaze was directed away from confrontation — not at the viewer, not at her cubs, but into the distance. That single choice carries the whole painting: calm vigilance rather than aggressive confrontation. Several eye-direction studies preceded the final, settled read.

## Colour and composition

- Warm oranges and golds — the late-afternoon golden hour.
- Cool blues in shadow — atmospheric depth, keeps the scene from feeling hot.
- Deep earth tones — grounding, connecting the family to their environment.

The mother stands between the viewer and her cubs (protective, not aggressive). The cub is partially hidden behind her — instinctive protection and curiosity at once, the tension between safety and exploration.

## Light behaviour (visual spine of the video)

- **4 PM** — flat light, colours muted.
- **6 PM** — golden hour, oranges and golds glow warmly.
- **7 PM** — deep shadows, a protective atmosphere emerges.

## Where it belongs

A west-facing living room, a quiet study, a reading nook with evening light — somewhere the late sun reaches the canvas for an hour each day. Painted for the stillness seeker: someone who values quiet moments, family bonds, and calm in their space.

## Visual cues for the video

- Slow push-in on the mother's averted gaze; let the viewer feel she is watching something beyond the frame.
- A real-time light sweep across the canvas from 4 PM flat → 6 PM golden glow → 7 PM deep shadow.
- Close detail on the cub half-hidden behind the mother, then pull back to the full family grouping.
- Ambient sound bed: gentle wind, distant bird calls at sunset, leaves rustling — the actual signature interaction.
- Closing hero shot in golden-hour light with the studio line: *every painting eventually leaves the studio; the story it carries never does.*

## How it connects to the collection

Tiger Family anchors the Wildlife Room — beside it, a close tiger portrait (restraint, whisker dots applied with the brush handle) and Cranes at Sunset (the room's exhale, one untouched wash). Together they argue that wildlife here is observed with patience, not decorated.

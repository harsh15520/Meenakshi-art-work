## Portrait-set summary

Five Faces We Come From is a commissioned family set — five graphite portraits, each 12 × 16 in — made so a hallway could hold the people a family comes from: the parents, the in-laws, and the grandmother who raised them all. It isn't one likeness; it's five, drawn to read as one wall.

## The brief

A daughter asked for five graphite portraits from family photographs — the parents, the husband's parents, and the grandmother who raised her — to hang together in a hallway. In her words, she wanted to pass them every morning and remember who she comes from. The testimonial that opened the commission: *"These five are my parents and my husband's parents and the grandmother who raised us all. Every morning I pass them and remember who I come from."* The set wasn't decorative — it kept the grandmother and both sets of parents present in daily life.

## The temperature problem

Five photographs from different decades, lighting and quality all over the place. The risk was five portraits that looked like five different artists. They had to read as one family, hung as one wall, not a collage of strangers.

## Two decisions that held it together

- **The Diwali photographs won** — everyone looked happiest and most themselves; the formal studio shots were rejected, too stiff, reading as ID photos.
- **Soft living-room light** — soft graphite as one material across all five keeps the wall unified; bare-paper highlights kept cool and unmarked so the set feels lit by the home, not the studio; a single warm mat board ties the five faces into one wall.

## The 6-week build

The notebook marks it plainly: Day 2, draw the grandmother first — she's the anchor the others hang around. Day 9, keep every face soft, like the client's living-room light, not a sharp studio. Six weeks from brief to delivery in Delhi.

## The detail most people miss

All five were drawn with the same hand-pressure range, so they read as one set from across the hall — this shared pressure is the visual star. And the grandmother's precedence: drawn first and centred in the planning, even though she hangs among equals, the quiet hierarchy of the wall.

## What should the video focus on?

Create a quiet, cinematic walkthrough of the gallery painting **"Radha-Krishna in Gold & Blue — Blue, and the Gold That Follows"** (acrylic, 24 × 30 in) that feels like dusk settling into devotion. Open on the idea that this painting is painted not as fire but as dusk — blue settling, gold answering, two figures who don't need to face each other to be together. Explain the temperature call: the moonlit version was too cool and lost the earthly warmth, the theatre duet too loose for the centre wall, and the chosen warm midday blue stays luminous rather than dark. Make the central technique the visual star — the gold-first, blue-float move: the warm gold halo laid first, then the luminous blue floated wet-on-wet over its edge so the two breathe, because gold-first is what keeps the blue alive on a dark field, and a touch of rose keeps Radha human. Show the dusk-to-night light — morning halo as pale fire, noon gold and blue equal, evening halo glowing as the blue deepens. Reveal the silent symbols most people walk past: the flute resting not raised (the pause between notes) and the near-touching hands (restrained devotion). End on where it belongs — above a console where the evening lamp catches the gold at the door — with the studio's "story it carries never does" line. Keep the edit unhurried and reverent, matching the painting's sacred stillness.

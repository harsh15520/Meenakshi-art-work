## What should the video focus on?

Create an immersive, cinematic walkthrough of the commissioned gate **"Peacock Courtyard Gate — The Gate That Says Welcome"** (carved wood, 6 × 4 ft, private courtyard entrance). Open with the idea that some commissions are about a thing and this one was about an entrance — the first object every guest meets, built to say "welcome" the way a family actually means it. Weave in the client's own words (welcome with live birdsong and flowers) and the wedding-invitation peacocks as a love story carved at the threshold. Explain the central craft problem — a carved gate and a painted gate are two different crafts, so carving had to do structure and paint had to do life without fighting, answered by carved seasoned wood sealed with layered acrylic on the relief. Compress the five-week build into a time-lapse (carve arch + neem trunk → peacocks in relief underpainted → blossoms + sealing → Saharanpur installation in morning light). Make morning sunlight on the relief the visual spine — the moment the carved peacocks read alive. Spotlight the two details people miss: the relief peacock eyes carved a few millimetres proud so they catch sun, and the neem whose branches form the arch so the gate is the tree. End on the living gate — guests pausing and photographing it before the house, the neem leaves shifting through the day, the family's "we're home" marker — with the studio's "this one was about an entrance" line. Keep it warm, tender, and unhurried.

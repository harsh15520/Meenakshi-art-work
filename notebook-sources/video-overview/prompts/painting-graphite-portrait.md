## What should the video focus on?

Create a slow, cinematic walkthrough of the gallery painting **"Graphite Portrait — A Face in Pencil Only"** (graphite, 12 × 16 in) that feels like a hand pressing, not a colour applied. Open on the idea that this portrait proves a likeness with pressure — a single grey field, graded by how hard the pencil bore down — instead of with colour. Explain the colour problem: a graphite portrait risks reading as a study, not finished, as if colour "hasn't been added yet," and how this brief made monochrome feel complete for a buyer who wanted character without decoration. Show the key turn — pressure as pigment — where the whole face is carried by one gradient from the dark jawline to the pale chin, and how layering strokes made it muddy until a single pressure-gradient cleared it. Reveal the materials (graphite 2B through 6B on cold-press paper) and why charcoal, coloured pencil, and digital were rejected. Demonstrate the two quiet techniques: lifted-pressure hatching (strokes rising off the paper so the line fades without a hard stop) and negative drawing for highlights (lightest areas left as bare paper, staying cool while shadows warm). End on the detail most miss — the likeness in one jawline, the bare-paper brightest spots — and the ideal owner who reads a drawing as drawing. Keep the edit calm and monochrome, matching the painting's own restraint.

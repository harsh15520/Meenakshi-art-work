## What should the video focus on?

Create a quiet, cinematic walkthrough of the commissioned painting **"The Morning Corner — The First Face of the Morning"** (acrylic, 2 × 3 ft, for the Sharma family's home prayer corner), built around a single true anchor: it was painted from one family photograph, for one specific corner, to be the first face they see each morning — not a generic deity painting. Open on the grandmother's tradition as the client's own voice, since no separate testimonial exists: she always said Krishna should be the first face seen in the morning. State plainly that the painting comes from a single reference — the family's own temple photograph, the deity they grew up with — rather than implying a shortlist of options; the personal, single-source nature of the reference is the point, not a weakness to dress up. Walk through the two mid-painting turns as a pair: day three, the client asking that Krishna's eyes sit at prayer-mat height so the figure meets the family where they kneel; day twelve, gold pigment test #3 finally reading true and warm in morning light. Spend real time on the four details that reward a close look, each with its actual meaning: the peacock crown worked with the artist's own signature motif, placed to catch first light before anything else in the room; the marigold and lotus offerings painted from the real flowers the family sets there weekly; the flute in one hand and the quiet Om in the other, two sounds that open the day; and the lowered, prayer-mat-height gaze, the first face a child sees at dawn. Center the emotional close on the 6 AM installation itself — the family watching the first light strike the gold crown exactly as painted — then carry the story forward past the hanging: the prayer mat moved three times to find the 6:15 gold spot, fresh Friday marigolds, the mother praying there and remembering her childhood temple, the lamp moved three weeks later, the daughter lighting her own diya two months later. Do not reference any light-simulator, interactive time-of-day tool, or multi-reference gallery — none of those exist for this painting; the light story is told through the installation and the living-with-it moments, not an interactive feature. End on the studio's "story it carries never does" line. Keep it unhurried and devotional, and let the four specific details, not generic morning-light imagery, carry the video's texture.

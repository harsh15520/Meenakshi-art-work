## What should the video focus on?

Create an immersive cinematic walkthrough of the gallery painting **"Elephant · Pattern Art — The Elephant That Marches Off the Wall"** (acrylic, 2 × 3 ft) built around a single reveal: this is not a decorated animal, it's a procession you're watching arrive. Open tight on the finished painting reading as pure ornament, then let the viewer realize it's moving — crimson banding across the head and back, olive settling into the legs, a sunburst blazing behind the raised trunk, looping medallion arcs framing it left and right. State the trap the studio refused: cover a whole animal in pattern and you risk wallpaper — pretty, but you forget there's an elephant underneath — and the buyer wanted ornament and animal at once. Make the *build order* the visual spine of the video, rebuilt on screen as three literal passes: Pass One, the cobalt-blue road laid down first, cool and still, broken only by green medallion arcs; Pass Two, a single bending current drawn from trunk to tail before any decoration — the direction the whole painting will obey; Pass Three, the crowd arriving — crimson-to-olive cells dropping in one by one, each hand-set at its own angle, following that current. Show the straight-grid-vs-bent-grid comparison directly, so the "why bend it" logic reads visually: a flat tiled version goes dead, then the grid curves to the trunk's curl and the haunch's round and the elephant reappears underneath the ornament. Give the sunburst its own beat — the one blazing point the procession moves toward — and the medallion arcs their own beat — the gates the procession passes through. State the real palette plainly: crimson red as the ceremonial body, cobalt blue as the ground it crosses, sunburst gold-orange as the single hot accent, olive green settling the legs to earth. Do not describe a teal ground, marigold motifs, bells, or feather-eyes — none of those are in the actual canvas. Close in on the trunk's single spiral, using the dedicated trunk-detail crop, showing the one place the banding breaks into a curl — a breath in the procession — then pull back to the full elephant in motion. End on who it's for — a patient viewer who reads slowly and wants a festival on the wall, not a picture — with the studio's "story it carries never does" line. Keep the edit ornate but always legible: the three-pass build and the bending grid are the memorable beats, not just texture on an elephant.

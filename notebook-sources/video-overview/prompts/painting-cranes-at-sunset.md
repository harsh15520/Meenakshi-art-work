## What should the video focus on?

Create a slow, breath-held cinematic walkthrough of the gallery painting **"Cranes at Sunset — The Hour the Cranes Cross"** (acrylic, 2 × 1.5 ft) that feels like a moment held rather than a subject captured. Open on the idea that this painting holds a moment — the few seconds at dusk when cranes cross the last light and the water goes perfectly still. Explain the central break: the stillness problem, the bird-in-flight action cliché (wings spread, motion blurred) that the brief deliberately refused, wanting near-suspended calm that lowers the room's pulse instead of raising it. Show the key move — the water laid in a single wet wash and left entirely alone so the cranes float on stillness, and how the discipline was *no hard edge anywhere*, with early ripples and reflections removed because each only made the scene busier. Reveal the last ten minutes of light: amber sky (the exact amber before sunset, not a generic orange), pale rose water (the sky's warmth cooled and flattened into the wash), and the soft graphite cranes kept nearly monochrome so they read as silhouette, not specimen. Let the light behaviour be the visual spine — Midday flat warm, 6 PM the sky and water meet and the cranes lift, Dusk the whole piece dims to a single glowing band. End on where it belongs — a corridor end, above a bath, a west-facing wall — with the studio's "story it carries never does" line. Keep the edit slow and unhurried, matching the painting's own calm.

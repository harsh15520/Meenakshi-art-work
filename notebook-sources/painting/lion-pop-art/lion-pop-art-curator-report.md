## The story

Some paintings observe an animal. This one declares one — flat colour, hard edges, a stare borrowed more from print than from the wild.

A lion almost has to be painted realistic: mane, fur, savannah, the whole nature documentary. But that reflex was exactly what this brief wanted to break. The buyer wasn't asking for another faithful animal; they wanted range on the wall, a deliberate break from the rest of the collection.

## The technique

The turn was to treat the lion as a graphic object. The mane wasn't drawn stroke by stroke — it was built from repeated stencil-like shapes. The pattern itself became the subject, and the lion became a poster. Free-drawn manes kept drifting back toward realism no matter how many times it was attempted; it was only when a fixed, repeated unit was locked in that the graphic read finally held.

The colour is loud and unapologetic — four of them, no more. Signal orange is the body, the first thing the eye lands on. Cobalt blue fills the negative space and pushes that orange forward, keeping the poster feel. Hot magenta adds the third pop on the mane so it never settles into just two tones. And a black outline holds every shape — hard edges, never painterly.

Technique served the language. Each colour was blocked in clean with masked flat fills, no blending across the boundaries, because blending would betray the print. Every edge got the same black line at the same weight; that equal weight is what makes it read as a poster and not a sketch.

## The detail most people miss

It is the only painting in the Pop Room where repetition is the subject. The mane's stencil-like units aren't decoration on top of a lion — they *are* the lion. Step back and the repeated shapes resolve into a face; step in and you see the discipline of the repeat.

## Living with it

This one belongs where energy is wanted more than calm — a young person's room, a studio corner, an entrance that should pop. It is for someone who wants the wall to speak first: bold, graphic, unapologetic, a deliberate break from the rest of the collection.

# Mother & Child — Infographic Spec · "The Quiet Between Them"

> **Source type:** Gallery painting infographic (one-page cheat-sheet) · **Slug:** `mother-and-child-blessing`
> **Painting:** Mother & Child · Oil, 2 × 1.5 ft · **Layout:** portrait / square; landscape OK
> **Companion source:** `mother-and-child-blessing-curator-report.md`

## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how a hush becomes a painting" — escaping the sweetness cliché, the lowered gaze, the lamplight palette, and the suspended blessing hand.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The move in one line
- Describe a feeling → **hold** one: the wordless hush between a mother and a child before either speaks.

### 2. The sweetness problem (what it avoids)
- Mother-and-child cliché = round cheeks, glowing halos, everything resolved, safe and pretty.
- This brief breaks the reflex: a feeling remembered from childhood, not a nice wall picture.
- Why: the buyer wanted a quiet they couldn't name brought home.

### 3. Lowering the gaze (the key move)
- Mother's eyes rest down on the child, never the viewer.
- That single choice turns a portrait into a private moment we only overhear.
- Three studies tried eye contact (greeting-card feel); the lowered gaze was the fourth and felt true.

### 4. Warmth without heat (swatch grid + roles)
- **Soft ochre ground** — the room's warmth; keeps the scene in lamplight temperature, not sunlight.
- **Muted rose on the child** — tenderness; a blush, never bright, so the child reads as held not highlighted.
- **Deep umber shadow** — grounding; pulls the figures in, enclosing the moment instead of staging it.

### 5. The blessing hand (symbolism strip)
- **Raised hand, not touching** — a blessing held in suspension, deliberately unfinished in its reach.
- **Child's fist in the dupatta** — the only tense line; where love becomes physical.

### 6. What people almost miss
- Blurred blessing hand: barely painted up close, resolves into a hand at distance; the blur is the point.
- The single hard line: the child's fist — tenderness becoming grip.

### 7. How it lives
- Hallway, hung low — children point first.
- Bedroom — night-light companion, warm ground glows without glare.
- Guests go quiet, then say "oh."

## Visual cues
- Hero crop: the mother's lowered gaze and the hovering blessing hand (`/images/custom/custom-work-1.webp`).
- Three warm colour blocks labelled by role (ochre ground / muted rose / deep umber shadow); no bright accents — keep the temperature soft.
- A light-simulator strip: Morning (cool window light, ochre stays quiet) → Afternoon (full warmth, rose blooms) → Lamp-lit evening (ground glows, moment enclosed).
- Use soft edges and an enclosed frame throughout — the infographic should *feel* like the painting's quiet.

## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how an elephant becomes pattern" — escape the wallpaper trap, the square-by-square mapping, the cool-teal-and-marigold palette, and the one detail everyone misses: the trunk's spiral.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The move in one line
- Decorate a subject → **become** the subject: an elephant built entirely from the patterns laid across its body.

### 2. The flatness problem (what it avoids)
- Pattern cliché = cover the animal, lose the animal — wallpaper, not a beast.
- This brief refuses that: ornament AND animal, neither at the other's expense.
- Why: the buyer wanted both at once.

### 3. Mapping square by square (the key move)
- Pattern mapped cell by cell, every motif tilted to follow the body's curves.
- Decoration reveals the shape instead of hiding it.
- A straight grid flattened the creature; bending the grid with the form brought the animal back.

### 4. Cool field, warm motifs (swatch grid)
- **Teal ground** — the body mass; cool and calm so warm motifs pop.
- **Marigold motifs** — the pattern; a single warm note, repeated, echoing devotional work.
- **Cream outline** — the separation; keeps each cell legible without a hard black edge.

### 5. One cell at a time (technique strip)
- **Hand-placed motifs** — no two at the same angle; variation reads as made (uniform repeat reads as printed).
- **Curved grid** — bends to follow the trunk and haunch; keeps the elephant readable through the ornament.

### 6. The detail most miss
- **The trunk's spiral** — the one place the repeat breaks into a single curl, a breath in the ornament.
- **The marigolds** — flower of welcome and worship; this elephant is dressed for a festival, not a zoo.

## Visual cues
- Hero crop: the elephant's trunk resolving into the single spiral (`/images/painting/oil-painting-82.webp`).
- Three palette swatches labelled teal / marigold / cream; a "no hard edge" cue (cream outline, not black).
- Show the bending grid as faint lines curving along the trunk and haunch, so the form reappears.
- Keep the layout ornamented but legible — the infographic should *feel* like the painting without becoming wallpaper.

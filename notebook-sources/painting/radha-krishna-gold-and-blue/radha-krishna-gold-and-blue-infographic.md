## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how dusk becomes devotion" — the three-reference temperature call, the gold-first / blue-float technique, the exact blue-gold-rose palette, and the silent symbols most people walk past.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The move in one line
- Painted as fire → **painted as dusk**: blue settling, gold answering, two figures who don't need to face each other to be together.

### 2. The temperature call (what it avoids)
- Moonlit version: too cool — lost the earthly warmth the buyer wanted.
- Theatre duet: lovely but too loose for the centre wall.
- **Chosen:** a midday study in a warm blue that stays luminous, not dark.

### 3. Gold first, blue second (the key move)
- The Day 2 rule: gold first, blue second — reverse it and the halo dies.
- Warm gold halo laid first; luminous blue floated wet-on-wet over its edge so the two breathe.
- Why: the blue is the hardest pigment to keep alive on a dark field; laying gold first lets it live.

### 4. Three colours, exact (swatch grid + hex)
- **Luminous Blue `#2a3a6a`** — the night around them; mixed to glow, not recede.
- **Warm Gold `#c9a227`** — devotion made visible; laid first so the blue floats over its edge.
- **Rose `#b86b7a`** — Radha's earthly warmth; keeps her human amid the divine blue.

### 5. Where the gold catches (light strip)
- **Morning, east window** — halo reads as pale fire; blue stays recessive.
- **Noon, flat light** — gold and blue reach equal weight (most balanced read).
- **Evening, lamp** — the halo glows; the blue deepens into night.

### 6. The detail most miss (symbols)
- **Flute at the side, resting not raised** — music implied; the pause between notes.
- **Near-touching hands** — fingers almost meet but don't; restrained devotion, not union.

### 7. Where it belongs
- Above a console, evening lamp lit before guests, gold catching at the door.
- Settings: pooja-room antechamber · dining console · stair landing.
- For: someone who knows the lore, likes warmth over spectacle, wants a daily not a display.

## Visual cues

- A small dusk-to-night light bar (morning → noon → evening) to show the gold catch.
- Mark the resting flute (x30, y60) and near-touching hands (x50, y55) with callouts.

## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how three devotional paintings become one finished corner" — the brief, the three-not-one problem, the two client requests, the shared-gold resolution, and the corner's daily life.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The commission at a glance (fact strip)
- **Subject:** Marigold Temple Deity (part of a 3-painting puja corner: temple deity · Radha-Krishna · mother-and-child blessing)
- **Medium:** Acrylic
- **Size:** 2 × 1.5 ft
- **For:** A family home · **Completed in:** 5 weeks · **Delivered:** Haridwar · **Price:** ₹4,000

### 2. The brief
- Three devotional paintings for a home puja corner, hung so the space felt finished.
- Client voice: *"Our puja corner finally feels finished. The diya is lit every morning and the paintings catch the light first thing."*

### 3. The problem (three, not one)
- A set of three devotional works can feel like three separate commissions.
- The challenge: compose them as one corner — related, not repeated.
- The client wanted a single feeling of completion, not three pretty pictures above a shrine.

### 4. Two requests that shaped it (callouts)
- **The morning diya** — could the diya light hit the paintings first thing, because that's when the corner matters most; the gold is composed to catch the early flame.
- **Facing the entrance** — Radha-Krishna faces the entrance to greet anyone who comes in; the hang was reordered so the greeting deity meets you at the threshold.

### 5. The turn — one composed corner
- Three standalone icons rejected: looked like three commissions, never cohered.
- One composed corner chosen: related palettes and a shared gold so the three read as a single devotion.

### 6. The palette
- **Warm gold** — the unifying thread; each painting carries gold so the corner reads as one devotion in morning light.
- **Deep marigold** — the deity's flower; ties the temple deity to the welcome tradition.
- **Soft rose** — the blessing figures; keeps mother-and-child and Radha-Krishna human amid the divine.

### 7. Living in the corner
- The diya is lit every morning; the gold catches it first thing.
- Radha-Krishna greets anyone entering; the mother-and-child blessing became the focal point of daily prayer.

## Visual cues
- A faint three-frame corner sketch (temple deity · Radha-Krishna · mother-and-child) with one gold line drawn continuously across all three edges.
- A small diya at lower-left with light rays angled to meet the gold at dawn position (6am).
- A marigold flower marker at the deity's feet, labelled "welcome / receive."
- Entrance arrow pointing toward Radha-Krishna to show the greeting orientation.
- A soft warm-gold / marigold / rose swatch strip along the bottom.

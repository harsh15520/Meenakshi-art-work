## The story

Some commissions fill a wall. This one finished a corner — three devotional paintings brought together so a home's puja space could feel, at last, complete.

The family's request set the brief: three devotional paintings for a home puja corner — a temple deity, a Radha-Krishna, and a mother-and-child blessing — hung so the space felt finished. In the client's own words, they wanted the corner to feel complete: *"Our puja corner finally feels finished. The diya is lit every morning and the paintings catch the light first thing."*

## The technique

The hard part was never the painting — it was the number. A set of three devotional works can easily feel like three separate commissions. The challenge was to compose them as one corner — related, not repeated. The client wanted a single feeling of completion, not three pretty pictures above a shrine.

Two client requests shaped the entire composition. First, the morning diya: could the diya light hit the paintings first thing in the morning, because that's when the corner matters most? The work was composed so the gold catches the early flame — the corner built around the daily lamp, not the room's main light. Second, facing the entrance: Radha-Krishna was to face the entrance so they greet anyone who comes in. The hang was reordered so the greeting deity meets you at the threshold of the corner.

The composition evolved from three standalone icons — which looked like three commissions and never cohered — to one composed corner, with related palettes and a shared gold so the three read as a single devotion. The palette carries warm gold as the unifying thread each painting shares so the corner reads as one devotion in morning light; deep marigold ties the temple deity to the welcome tradition of the flower; soft rose keeps the mother-and-child and Radha-Krishna human amid the divine.

## The detail most people miss

The gold line is doing the structural work. A single gold edge runs across all three paintings so the corner reads as one devotion — the eye travels the whole corner, not three separate frames. And the marigold at the deity's feet is placed as the flower of welcome and worship, so the corner is dressed to receive, not just to display. The notebook captures it plainly: paint the gold to meet the diya's morning angle — the corner lives at 6am, not noon; and one gold line across all three so they're a set, not a row.

## Living with it

The diya is lit every morning and the gold catches it first thing, exactly as asked. Radha-Krishna greets anyone entering the corner, as the family wanted. The mother-and-child blessing became the focal point of daily prayer. What was an unfinished shelf above a shrine is now a corner the family starts its day in.

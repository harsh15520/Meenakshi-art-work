# Occasion Portrait — Infographic Spec · "The Way She Looked That Day"

> **Source type:** Commissioned portrait infographic (one-page cheat-sheet) · **Slug:** `occasion-portraits-wedding`
> **Painting:** Occasion Portrait · Graphite, 12 × 16 in · **Layout:** portrait (tall) preferred
> **Companion source:** `occasion-portraits-wedding-curator-report.md`

## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how a wedding occasion portrait gets made" — the brief, the details problem, the two client requests, the 3-week build, the four-colour palette, and the details that carry the day.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The commission at a glance (fact strip)
- **Subject:** Occasion Portrait · Floral Pin (a daughter's wedding)
- **Medium:** Graphite on warm-toned paper
- **Size:** 12 × 16 in
- **Occasion:** Wedding · **Gift from:** A mother · **Completed in:** 3 weeks · **Price:** ₹9,000

### 2. The brief
- Styled graphite portraits for a daughter's wedding.
- Capture the jewellery, the embroidery, and the way her hair was pinned — so the look of that day is kept.
- Client voice: *"She wanted portraits that felt like the way she looked that day."*

### 3. The problem
- Drawing the details, not just the face.
- A wedding portrait fails if the jhumkas, embroidery and hairpin melt into "suggested" detail.
- Render the occasion, not just the person — those details were the whole point.

### 4. Two requests that shaped it (callouts)
- **The sangeet jhumkas** — include the earrings she wore at the sangeet; drawn as a named detail, not a generic stud, so the portrait names the celebration.
- **The venue light** — a background soft and warm, like the wedding venue; the ground shifted to a warm wash so it carries the room, not a studio grey.

### 5. Material
- **Graphite on warm-toned paper**, embroidery picked out in **fine white**.
- Keeps it intimate and drawn; warm paper + white highlights stop it reading as a cold ID sketch.
- Rejected: full colour (too photo-like) · pure charcoal (lost fine jewellery) · digital (no hand behind it).

### 6. The 3-week timeline (horizontal strip)
- **W1** Reference & likeness (face and expression from wedding photos) → **W2** Jewellery & embroidery (jhumkas, hairpin, threadwork) → **W3** Background & finishing (warm wash), delivered before the memories faded.

### 7. The four-colour palette (color-palette-explorer)
| Swatch | Hex | Name | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⬤ | `#c9a227` | Gold Jewellery | The jhumkas and hairpin — the festive anchor of the whole portrait. |
| ⬤ | `#b86b7a` | Rose Embroidery | The bridal threadwork — ties the portrait to the wedding palette. |
| ⬤ | `#f3e6d2` | Warm Paper | The ground — keeps the likeness intimate, not clinical. |
| ⬤ | `#2f6b4f` | Emerald Accent | A single bangle — the one cool note that makes the warm read as celebration. |

### 8. Details people almost miss
- **The named jhumkas** — sangeet earrings drawn specifically, not generic studs; the client's real request, preserved.
- **The embroidery thread** — blouse threadwork followed stitch by stitch; up close, clearly hand-drawn, not printed.

## Visual cues
- Tall 12 × 16 in frame motif; a soft, warm ground wash behind the head instead of a hard studio grey.
- The gold jhumka drawn first as the festive focal point (per the notebook: "draw the jhumka first").
- A zoom callout on the embroidery thread showing individual stitches — the hand behind it.
- Four-swatch palette strip with hex chips (gold, rose, warm paper, emerald).

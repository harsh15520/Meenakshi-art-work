# The Morning Corner — Infographic Spec · "The First Face of the Morning"

> **Source type:** Commissioned painting infographic (one-page cheat-sheet) · **Slug:** `morning-corner-krishna`
> **Painting:** The Morning Corner · Krishna in a Gold Crown · Acrylic, 2 × 3 ft · **Layout:** portrait / square; landscape OK

## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how a home's first morning face gets made" — the grandmother's tradition, the chosen family reference, the 6/9/12 morning-light strip, the gold-pigment turn, and the two details most people miss.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The commission at a glance (fact strip)
- **Subject:** Krishna in a Gold Crown (The Morning Corner)
- **Medium:** Acrylic
- **Size:** 2 × 3 ft
- **For:** The Sharma Family · Home prayer corner · Price — ₹10,000

### 2. The grandmother's tradition (emotional core)
- Grandmother always said Krishna should be the first face seen each morning.
- The painting honors that — a home opening its eyes to Krishna first.
- No separate testimonial; the client voice is the client-memory module itself.

### 3. The reference choice (callouts)
- **Chosen:** the family's own temple photograph — a living deity they grew up with.
- **Rejected:** a generic stock temple reference — too generic, no personal thread.

### 4. The morning-light strip (time bar: 6 → 9 → 12)
- **6 AM — First light:** the first ray hits the gold crown; warm focal point of the corner.
- **9 AM — Full morning:** east-facing windows fill the room; the whole painting glows.
- **12 PM — Midday:** overhead light reveals the depth of a 15-layer background.

### 5. The turn (two changes to the face)
- **Day 3:** Krishna's eyes set at prayer-mat height — the figure meets you where you kneel.
- **Day 12:** gold pigment test #3 with warm undertones finally reads true in morning light.

### 6. The details most people miss (zoom callouts)
- **The averted gaze:** eyes never look directly at you — gently lowered, reverent, non-confronting.
- **The lotus at his feet:** not in the reference; added for the grandmother who loved lotuses — her presence in the prayer.

### 7. Living in the corner (timeline notes)
- Installed at 6 AM in the exact light it was painted for; first light struck the gold crown as intended.
- Prayer mat moved three times to catch the 6:15 gold; fresh marigolds every Friday.
- Mother prays there (gold reminds her of her childhood temple); lamp moved opposite at 3 weeks; daughter lights the diya by 2 months — now her morning duty.

## Visual cues
- Hero crop: the gold crown catching the 6 AM first ray (`/images/exhibition/1.webp` / `/images/painting/oil-painting-93.webp`).
- A horizontal light-simulator time strip with three sun positions (6 / 9 / 12) and warm-to-full glow gradient.
- Zoom insets on the lowered eyes and the foot-lotus.
- Gold palette swatch for the crown; a small "warm undertones" pigment chip noting test #3.

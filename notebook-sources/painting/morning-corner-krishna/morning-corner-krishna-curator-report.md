## The story

Some homes begin their day with silence. This one begins with light. A corner where incense meets morning sun, where bells ring before the world wakes, where gold catches the first ray of day.

The Sharma family came with a tradition, not just a request. Their grandmother always said Krishna should be the first face you see in the morning, and this painting honors that. It was built from the family's own temple photograph — the deity they had grown up with. There's no separate testimonial for this commission; the client's voice is the grandmother's tradition itself, carried into the brief by the family.

## The technique

Two requests shaped the face. On day three the client asked that Krishna's eyes sit at prayer-mat height, so the figure meets the family where they kneel. On day twelve the gold pigment finally behaved — test number three, with warm undertones, the one that reads true in morning light.

## The details most people miss

The gold crown is worked with tiny peacock plumes — the artist's signature motif — placed to catch the first morning ray before anything else in the room. Marigold and lotus garlands rest at the base, painted from the real flowers the family places there each week. In one hand, the flute; in the other, the quiet Om — two symbols of the sound that opens the day in this home. And Krishna's eyes are set at prayer-mat height, so the first face a child sees at dawn is the one their grandmother asked for.

## Living with it

The painting was delivered and installed at 6 AM so the client could see it in the exact light it was painted for; together they watched the first morning light strike the gold crown as intended. The family moved their prayer mat three times before finding the spot where the light hits the gold at 6:15. Fresh marigolds now arrive every Friday as part of the weekly ritual. The client's mother visits just to pray there, saying the gold reminds her of the temple she grew up visiting. Three weeks later they moved the lamp to the opposite wall because evening light made it glow better. Two months later the daughter learned to light the diya herself — it is now her morning responsibility.

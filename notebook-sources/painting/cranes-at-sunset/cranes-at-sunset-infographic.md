# Cranes at Sunset — Infographic Spec · "The Hour the Cranes Cross"

> **Source type:** Gallery painting infographic (one-page cheat-sheet) · **Slug:** `cranes-at-sunset`
> **Painting:** Cranes at Sunset · Acrylic, 2 × 1.5 ft · **Layout:** landscape OK; square / portrait OK
> **Companion source:** `cranes-at-sunset-curator-report.md`

## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how a moment becomes a painting" — refuse the action cliché, lay one untouched wash, keep no hard edge, and let the last ten minutes of light do the work.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The move in one line
- A subject → **a moment**: cranes crossing the last light, the water perfectly still.

### 2. The stillness problem (what it avoids)
- Bird-in-flight cliché = wings spread, motion blurred, the frame in a hurry.
- This brief refuses it: calm, near-suspended, the quiet before they land.
- Why: the buyer wanted a painting that lowers the room's pulse, not raises it.

### 3. One wash, never touched (the key move)
- Water = a single wet wash, laid and left entirely alone.
- The cranes float on stillness, not standing in it.
- Early ripples and reflections made it busier; removing them was the real work.
- The discipline: **no hard edge anywhere** in the piece.

### 4. The last ten minutes of light (swatch grid)
- **Amber sky** — the exact amber of the ten minutes before sunset, not a generic orange.
- **Pale rose water** — the sky's warmth, cooled and flattened into the wash as reflected calm.
- **Soft graphite cranes** — kept nearly monochrome so they read as silhouette, not specimen.

### 5. How it behaves in a room (light strip)
- **Midday** — flat warm light; the amber reads plain and even.
- **6 PM** — the sky and water meet; the cranes lift off the surface.
- **Dusk** — the whole piece dims to a single glowing band of light.

### 6. Where it belongs
- End of a corridor · above a bath · a west-facing wall.
- For: the stillness seeker — values calm, likes negative space, wants art that lowers the pulse.

## Visual cues
- Hero crop: the soft graphite cranes floating on the single untouched wash (`/images/exhibition/5.webp`).
- Three calm colour blocks — Amber, Pale rose, Soft graphite — labelled with their role, not just a name.
- A "no hard edge" icon: a soft, feathered boundary, the opposite of a crisp line.
- A light strip showing Midday → 6 PM → Dusk, ending on the single glowing band.
- Keep the infographic itself soft-edged and unhurried — it should *feel* like the painting.

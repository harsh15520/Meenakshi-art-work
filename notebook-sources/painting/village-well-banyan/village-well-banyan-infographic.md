## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how a remembered village becomes a painting" — escape the postcard, the dry-brush thread roots, the earth palette, and the kept chip.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The move in one line
- About a place → **about a routine**: the walk to the well, the wait in the banyan's shade.

### 2. The postcard problem (what it avoids)
- Village cliché = bright, flat 'colourful India' calendar art.
- This brief breaks the reflex: the weight of a remembered place, not a brochure.
- Why: the buyer grew up at such a well.

### 3. Painting the tree in threads (the key move)
- Banyan aerial roots = a nearly-empty brush, hundreds of separate dry strokes.
- Solid and whole from far; fragile threads up close — how real roots hang.

### 4. Earth, not rainbow (swatch grid)
- **Terracotta earth** — the actual village soil; the ground and the pots.
- **Dusty olive** — the banyan; muted green, old not decorative.
- **Pale sky wash** — left thin on purpose; the eye rests, never shouts.

### 5. Why the banyan, why the well
- The banyan = the village meeting place and institution where news is settled.
- The well = the day's first errand, the village's original social network.

### 6. The details people miss
- Dry-brush roots dissolve into individual strokes close; whole again from across the room.
- The chipped water pot, second from left, kept uncorrected on purpose.

### 7. Where it belongs
- A home far from the village; a small daily reminder in soft morning light.
- Dining room · stair landing · study.

## Visual cues
- Hero crop: the dry-brush banyan roots resolving into the tree, then pulling apart into individual strokes (`/images/painting/oil-painting-116.webp`).
- Three earth-tone blocks labelled terracotta / dusty olive / pale sky.
- A "kept chip" callout on the second pot from the left (`/images/painting/oil-painting-116.webp`).
- Use a quiet, matte, paper-like ground — the infographic should feel like the painting, not shout.

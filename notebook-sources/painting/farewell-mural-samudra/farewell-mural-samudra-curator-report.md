## The story

Some farewells are too large for words. This one needed a wall — an institutional hall where decades of service were honored not with a speech, but with mythology that speaks about effort itself.

The institution's request set the brief: a farewell mural for a mentor who had given the institution everything. It had to feel monumental — not just the achievement, but the effort itself. In the client's own words, they wanted *"something that would remind future generations of what persistence looks like."* And the testimonial that opened the commission: *"We commissioned this as a farewell gift for a mentor who gave the institution everything. It had to feel monumental — it had to hold the whole story."*

## The technique

The hard part was scale. What works on canvas doesn't always translate to mural scale — the figures needed to be readable from across a large hall while keeping detail up close. The adaptation was methodical: multiple scale studies to understand how figures would read from different distances, and viewing-angle tests from multiple points in the hall before any colour was committed.

Two client requests shaped the entire composition. First, that the serpent Vasuki wrap the whole composition — he was to be the backbone of the story, not a side detail. Second, that the emerging treasures appear gradually, not all at once: achievement as a process, not a moment. In the final work some treasures are fully visible and others are still forming.

The mural was built over eight weeks. Week 1 aligned on story and philosophy — why the churning metaphor mattered. Week 2 prepared and tested the institutional wall surface in sections before committing to the full 8 × 6 ft space. Weeks 3–4 resolved scale studies and figure composition for balance from multiple angles. Weeks 5–6 layered colour in mural acrylics formulated to withstand institutional lighting. Week 7 refined Vasuki and the emerging treasures. Week 8 brought installation, with the final touches added on the day of the farewell ceremony as students and faculty watched.

## The detail most people miss

The serpent is doing the structural work. Vasuki isn't a border ornament — he wraps the entire composition as its spine, the literal representation of effort itself. And the treasures' staged emergence is intentional: some complete, some becoming. Walk past quickly and it reads as a single mythological scene; slow down and the process of achievement reveals itself.

## Living with it

What was a blank, functional wall is now a teacher. Students pause in front of it on their way to class, some starting to research the story — mythology sparking curiosity. Faculty use it as a teaching example when discussing perseverance and collective effort. The mural has become a landmark for giving directions in the institution. The client's hope came true: future generations will see, on that wall, what persistence actually looks like.

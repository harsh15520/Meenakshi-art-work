## The story

Some commissions are for the wall. This one was for the table — hand-painted plates and a tray, sent from a daughter to her mother, meant to be used and washed, not stored and admired.

The daughter's request set the brief: a set of hand-painted wooden plates and a tray with jasmine and magnolia, sent to her mother for her birthday — beautiful, but safe to actually eat from. In her own words: *"She feeds us all on them now — the flowers match the jasmine in her garden."* And the testimonial that opened the commission: *"I sent these to my mother for her birthday. She feeds us all on them now — the flowers match the jasmine in her garden."*

Two client notes narrowed the work before anything was painted. First: *"Could the jasmine match the ones in my mother's garden — not a generic flower, her jasmine?"* Second: *"Please make the tray safe for actual food, not just display — she'll use it, not store it."*

## The technique

The hard part was holding two demands at once. Painted tableware usually fails one way: too precious to use, or too plain to love. This brief demanded both — food-safe and genuinely beautiful. The mother was going to use them daily; a display-only gift would have been a quiet insult.

The material answered both. Acrylic-painted wood, finished with a food-safe sealant: wood takes the floral colour warmly and, once sealed, survives washing and daily food. Ceramic was considered but set aside — the client wanted the lighter wood feel. Untreated wood is not food-safe. Printed melamine has no hand behind it.

The palette was built as three decisions. White jasmine was the daughter's request, matched to the mother's actual garden and not a generic bloom. Soft magnolia pink carried the companion plate — a second note, so the set reads as a pair rather than a repeat. Leaf green scrollwork does the joining work, carrying the eye around the rim so the flower sits in a garden instead of floating.

The set was made in four weeks. Week 1 was wood prep and base: plates and tray sealed and primed for paint. Weeks 2–3 were the florals, jasmine and magnolia painted by hand on each piece. Week 4 brought sealing and dispatch — the food-safe top coat applied, the set sent to Pune for the birthday. The notebook records both halves of the job plainly: Day 3, *match the jasmine to her garden photo, not a stock flower*; Day 18, *three coats of sealant — it has to survive a dishwasher, not just a shelf*.

## The detail most people miss

Two details, one visible and one not. The garden-matched jasmine was painted from the mother's actual garden photo — the daughter's one non-negotiable, and the reason the flower is recognisable rather than decorative. And the sealant coats: three coats of food-safe finish, invisible in the finished piece, but the reason the art survives the sink.

## Living with it

The set is in daily service. The mother serves the family on them daily — exactly as asked. The jasmine on the plate is now recognised as "hers" by visiting grandchildren. The tray became the birthday-table centre piece, then stayed. The brief asked for something beautiful that could be used; what came back is a set that gets used and is still looked at.

## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how a famous hill becomes a small, remembered monolith" — the memory-not-monument brief, the rejected hero composition, the warm-stone palette, and the dawn-to-dusk climb light.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The commission at a glance (fact strip)
- **Subject:** Shravanabelagola · Ruins & Monolith
- **Medium:** Acrylic
- **Size:** 2 × 3 ft
- **For:** A son, for his father · **Completed in:** 5 weeks · **Delivered:** Bengaluru
- **Price:** ₹10,000

### 2. The brief
- A painting of Shravanabelagola's ruins and monolith — NOT a postcard.
- The hill as the father remembers it, to hang above his bookshelf.
- Client voice: *"My father climbed this hill every year as a boy. This painting brings that hill back into his study."*

### 3. The concept that anchors it (callout)
- **Memory, not monument** — the monolith asked for "small, like a memory," the opposite of a hero-shot.
- Make a famous landmark feel intimate and remembered; a grand postcard would miss the point.
- The step where the father rested — a personal landmark among famous ones, named in the brief.

### 4. Big, then small (composition turn)
- **Rejected:** the hero composition — too postcard; the monolith dominated and the memory disappeared.
- **Chosen:** the intimate composition — the monolith sits small among the ruins, as the father would have seen it climbing.

### 5. The palette (colour chips)
- **Warm stone grey** — the ruins; the actual granite colour at golden hour, not cold grey.
- **Dusty green hillside** — the climb; keeps the scene grounded in a real place, not a backdrop.
- **Pale sky** — left thin so the eye rests on the small monolith.

### 6. The climb light (horizontal strip — the light-simulator spine)
- **Dawn** — ruins glow briefly; monolith a silhouette against first light.
- **Midday** — flat and honest; the climb as the father knew it.
- **Dusk** — the stone warms; the memory feels closest.

### 7. Details people almost miss
- **The small monolith** — painted intentionally small; the whole brief was "like a memory he's carrying," not a landmark.
- **The resting step** — flat step near the top, included because the father always rested there.

### 8. Living in the study
- Hung above the bookshelf where the father reads; the climb is now part of the room.
- Family conversations about boyhood climbs start with "look at the painting."
- The small monolith reads larger the longer you live with it — as memories do.

## Visual cues
- A small-frame motif with the monolith deliberately tiny among larger ruins to teach "small, like a memory."
- A faint division into three light states (Dawn / Midday / Dusk) echoing the light-simulator interaction.
- Highlight ring on the resting step so the eye finds the personal landmark among the famous ones.
- Colour chips in the exact palette order: warm stone grey → dusty green → pale sky.

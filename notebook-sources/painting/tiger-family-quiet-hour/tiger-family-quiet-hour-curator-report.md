## The story

Some paintings capture what they see. This one captures what they feel — the moment when the light changes, when the activity slows, when the world holds its breath. That is the hour this tiger family lives in.

The brief, unspoken as it was, set up a problem. Tiger paintings almost always reach for power and aggression: bared teeth, hunting poses, dominance. That is a true part of tigers, but not the whole story — and certainly not the one worth hanging on a wall at home. The challenge was to show protection, family bonds, and quiet strength without sliding into the cliché, while still keeping the essence of the animal intact.

## The technique

The resolution sat in the eyes. The mother's gaze was directed *away* from any confrontation — she is not looking at the viewer and not looking at her cubs, but into the distance. That single decision carries the entire painting: the watchfulness of protection, constant but calm. It was not arrived at in one stroke; several studies explored different eye directions before the calm-vigilance read won out over aggressive confrontation.

The palette was built around that hour. Warm oranges and golds hold the late-afternoon golden light; cool blues sit in the shadows to give atmospheric depth without making the scene feel hot; deep earth tones ground the family to their environment. The colour is the time of day as much as the subject.

## The detail most people miss

Look at where the mother stands — between you and her cubs. Not aggressive, not threatening, simply *there*. And the cub, half-hidden behind her, leaning into that shelter while its curiosity pulls it forward. That tension between safety and exploration is the quietest, truest thing in the frame, and it is easy to walk past.

## Living with it

The painting was made to catch warm evening light. At 4 PM the light is flat and the colours read muted; at 6 PM, golden hour, the oranges and golds glow; by 7 PM the shadows deepen and a protective atmosphere takes over. It imagines itself in a west-facing living room, a quiet study, or a reading nook with evening light — somewhere the late sun reaches the canvas for an hour each day. Whoever reads there will look up in their own quiet hour and find a kind of strength in this family's still presence.

This piece was painted for the stillness seeker — someone who values quiet moments, appreciates family bonds, and looks for calm in their space.

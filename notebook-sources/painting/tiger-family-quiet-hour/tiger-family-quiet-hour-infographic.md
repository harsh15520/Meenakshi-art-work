## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how to read the Tiger Family painting" — the problem it avoids, the gaze trick, the palette, the hidden symbolism, and how the light behaves through the evening.

## Sections to lay out (top → bottom or grid)

### 1. The problem it avoids
- Tiger cliché = power, aggression, bared teeth, hunting poses.
- This painting chooses protection, family bonds, quiet strength — without losing the animal's essence.

### 2. The gaze trick (the key move)
- Mother's eyes directed *into the distance*, not at viewer or cubs.
- Result: calm vigilance, not confrontation.
- Several eye-direction studies preceded the final choice.

### 3. Palette (3 swatches + role)
- **Warm oranges & golds** — late-afternoon golden hour.
- **Cool blues (shadow)** — atmospheric depth, keeps it from feeling hot.
- **Deep earth tones** — grounding, connects family to environment.

### 4. Hidden symbolism (2 callouts)
- **Mother's position** — stands between viewer and cubs: protective, not aggressive (how wild mothers actually guard young).
- **Cub's partial hiding** — instinctive protection + curiosity; the tension between safety and exploration.

### 5. Detail most people miss
- The mother isn't looking at you or the cubs — that distant gaze *is* the watchfulness of protection.

### 6. Light behaviour (time strip)
- **4 PM** — flat light, colours muted.
- **6 PM** — golden hour, oranges & golds glow.
- **7 PM** — deep shadows, protective atmosphere emerges.

### 7. Where it belongs
- West-facing living room · quiet study · reading nook with evening light.
- For: the stillness seeker (values quiet moments, family bonds, calm in their space).

## Visual cues
- Hero crop: the mother's face showing the averted gaze (`/images/painting/oil-painting-51.webp`).
- Three stacked colour bars for the palette; overlay a tiny sun-arc for the 4/6/7 PM strip.
- Use a calm, protective frame — no aggression iconography.

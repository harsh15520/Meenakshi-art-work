> **Companion source:** `family-keepsakes-graphite-curator-report.md`

## One-line concept

A single-page visual cheat-sheet for "how a five-portrait family set gets made" — the brief, the temperature problem, the two holding decisions, the 6-week build, and how the wall lives.

## Sections to lay out

### 1. The commission at a glance (fact strip)
- **Subject:** Five graphite family portraits (parents, in-laws, grandmother who raised the client)
- **Medium:** Graphite
- **Size:** 12 × 16 in (each)
- **Gift from:** A daughter · **Completed in:** 6 weeks · **Delivered:** Delhi · **Price:** ₹8,000 (per piece)

### 2. The brief
- Five graphite portraits from family photographs, to hang together in a hallway.
- It kept the grandmother who raised her, and both sets of parents, present in daily life.
- Client voice: *"Every morning I pass them and remember who I come from."*

### 3. The temperature problem
- Five photos from different decades; lighting and quality all over the place.
- Risk: five portraits that look like five different artists.
- Goal: read as one family, one wall — not a collage of strangers.

### 4. Two decisions that held it together (callouts)
- **The Diwali photos won** — everyone looked happiest and most themselves; formal studio shots rejected as too stiff, reading as ID photos.
- **Soft living-room light** — soft graphite as one material; bare-paper cool highlights; a single warm mat board ties the five faces into one wall.

### 5. The build (horizontal strip)
- **Brief** → **Diwali reference chosen** → **Day 2** draw grandmother first (anchor) → **Day 9** keep every face soft, living-room light → **6 weeks** delivered to Delhi.

### 6. The detail most miss
- **Unified pressure** — all five drawn with the same hand-pressure range, so they read as one set from across the hall.
- **Grandmother's precedence** — drawn first and centred in the planning, the quiet hierarchy of the wall.

### 7. Living in the hallway
- Hung at eye level so the family is met, not displayed.
- Visitors name the faces — reads as a family, not a gallery.
- The grandmother's portrait became the one guests ask about first.

## Visual cues
- Five portrait frames in a row motif; a faint warm-mat border unifying them.
- Bare-paper highlight zones left visibly unmarked (cool) against soft-graphite faces.
- A small "pressure range" bar showing one shared hand-pressure across all five.
- The centred frame marked as the grandmother — the quiet anchor of the row.

## Phase 03 — Acrylic Techniques

### Intro
Welcome back, friend — pour yourself a cup of chai, because Phase 03 is where we finally meet colour. This is Acrylic Techniques, and it's loud, fast, and gloriously forgiving.

### Focus
The promise here is colour, layering, and brush technique. We begin with colour-mixing discipline — a limited palette, and the golden rule that value comes before hue. Then brushwork variety: you'll meet the flat, the round, the filbert, and the fan, plus dry-brush and scumble. And finally layering and glazing with fast-drying acrylics, learning when to build opacity and when to let transparency sing.

### Tools
Your new companions are a joy. Acrylic paints — a primary set plus titanium white, burnt umber, and payne's grey. Synthetic brushes: a flat one-inch, a round 8, a filbert 6, and a fan, each a different voice. A palette knife gives you impasto and clean mixing. A 300 gsm acrylic paper or canvas pad survives all the layering, and water jars, a misting spray, and a stay-wet palette keep the paint workable.

### The Work
We start small: Primary Mixing, then Tint & Shade, a Value Scale in Paint, and a Limited Palette Portrait that forces harmony. You'll get acquainted with every brush — the flat for clean edges, the round for lines and dots, the filbert for soft leaf-like strokes, the fan for foliage. Then Dry-Brush, Scumble, Glazing, and Wet-on-Wet teach you to describe light and texture. An Apple Study holds the whole technique in one object, and a Simple Landscape and Still-Life in Colour bring it together before your Acrylic Final Piece.

### Outcome
What you leave with is real freedom: you can mix any colour you see, build a painting in confident layers, and use the brush to describe texture, edge, and light.

### Close
Don't fight the drying time — dance with it. Your brushes are waiting. See you in oils.

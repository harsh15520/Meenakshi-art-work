## Phase 02 — Proportions

### Intro
Hello again, and welcome back to the studio. Today we step into Phase 02 — and this is the phase where something almost magical happens: you stop drawing what you think you see, and you start seeing what's actually there.

### Focus
We call this one Proportions, and its heart is observation, balance, and composition. First, you'll learn to measure — sight-size, and the comparative method we call thumb-on-pencil, where your own hand becomes a ruler. Then comes negative space and the envelope: you'll block in the whole shape before any of the parts, which sounds strange but changes everything. And finally, compositional balance — the rule of thirds, visual weight, and the breathing room a good drawing needs.

### Tools
The toolkit builds on what you already own. You'll keep your HB through 6B graphite set. Printed reference photos at A4 give you a stable subject to measure against. Framing L's — little viewfinders — help you find the crop before you draw a single line. Toned charcoal paper gives you a mid-tone ground that makes value obvious, and a white charcoal pencil lets you recover highlights on that darker surface.

### The Work
The exercises train your eye more than your hand. You'll practise Sight-Size Measuring and Thumb Measuring, then draw The Envelope and a Negative Space Drawing — where the object appears correctly as a byproduct of drawing the spaces around it. Plumb Lines keep a face from drifting; Angle Checking describes form better than any outline. You'll try the Rule of Thirds, make Value Thumbnails, sketch a One-Point Interior, and even build a Two-Point Cube. Portrait Block-In, Hand Construction, and a Long Observational Study bring it all home.

### Outcome
And here's your reward: you can place anything accurately on the page — a face, a hand, a room — and arrange it so the composition feels balanced before you ever add a detail. The page becomes a window, not a wall.

### Close
Trust your eye. It's learning faster than you think. Rest now, and we'll meet again for colour.

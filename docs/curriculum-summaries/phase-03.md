# Phase 03 — Acrylic Techniques

## Overview
Phase 3, "Acrylic Techniques," is a seven-week dive into colour, layering, and brushwork, where the pencil gives way to paint. With 24 exercises and 42 student examples, students learn to mix any colour they see, build a painting in confident layers, and let the brush describe texture, edge, and light.

## Focus Areas
- Colour mixing discipline — limited palette, value before hue
- Brushwork variety — flat, round, filbert, fan; dry-brush and scumble
- Layering & glazing with fast-drying acrylics; building opacity vs transparency

## Tools Introduced
- Acrylic paints: primary set plus titanium white, burnt umber, payne's grey
- Synthetic brushes — flat 1", round 8, filbert 6, fan — each a different voice
- Palette knife — for impasto and mixing clean colour
- Acrylic paper / canvas pad, 300 gsm — survives layering and scrubbing
- Water jars, misting spray, stay-wet palette — keep acrylics workable

## Exercises

1. **Primary Mixing**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Mix secondary colours from primaries only.
   - What you learn: Every colour starts from three.
   - Success looks like: Clean secondaries without muddy grey.

2. **Tint & Shade**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Take one hue and make a tint/shade row.
   - What you learn: White and black extend a single hue.
   - Success looks like: Even steps from pale to deep.

3. **Value Scale in Paint**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Mix a 5-step grey value scale.
   - What you learn: Value reads before colour does.
   - Success looks like: Clear, even value steps.

4. **Limited Palette Portrait**
   - Duration: 30 min
   - What to do: Paint a small study with three colours only.
   - What you learn: Limits force harmony.
   - Success looks like: Convincing form from few pigments.

5. **Temperature Shift**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Mix warm and cool versions of one colour.
   - What you learn: Temperature creates daylight.
   - Success looks like: Warm and cool read distinctly.

6. **Brush Introduction: Flat**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Practise straight edges and broad washes with a flat.
   - What you learn: Flats lay even fields.
   - Success looks like: Clean edges, no streak gaps.

7. **Brush: Round**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Make lines, dots, and curves with a round.
   - What you learn: Rounds are the all-rounder.
   - Success looks like: Confident tapering lines and dots.

8. **Brush: Filbert**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Use a filbert for soft, leaf-like strokes.
   - What you learn: Filberts blend edges gently.
   - Success looks like: Soft, organic marks achieved.

9. **Brush: Fan**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Create foliage and texture with a fan brush.
   - What you learn: Fans suggest, rather than state.
   - Success looks like: Texture reads without overworking.

10. **Dry-Brush**
    - Duration: 20 min
    - What to do: Drag almost-dry paint for broken texture.
    - What you learn: Less paint means more surface.
    - Success looks like: Grainy, lively texture appears.

11. **Scumble**
    - Duration: 20 min
    - What to do: Scumble a thin light layer over a dry dark.
    - What you learn: Scumble adds atmosphere.
    - Success looks like: Hazy depth without muddiness.

12. **Glazing**
    - Duration: 25 min
    - What to do: Apply a transparent glaze to shift a colour.
    - What you learn: Glazes tune colour optically.
    - Success looks like: Colour shifts while staying luminous.

13. **Wet-on-Wet**
    - Duration: 20 min
    - What to do: Blend two acrylics while both are still wet.
    - What you learn: Acrylics blend fast — commit quickly.
    - Success looks like: Smooth transition before drying.

14. **Wet-on-Dry**
    - Duration: 20 min
    - What to do: Layer opaque strokes over a dry base.
    - What you learn: Dry layers keep crisp shapes.
    - Success looks like: Clean overlaps, no bleed.

15. **Palette Knife Marks**
    - Duration: 20 min
    - What to do: Lay thick paint with a knife for impasto.
    - What you learn: The knife gives physical texture.
    - Success looks like: Raised, expressive strokes.

16. **Colour Blocking**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Fill a canvas with flat colour zones first.
    - What you learn: Block big before small.
    - Success looks like: Balanced colour map of the scene.

17. **Monochrome Underpainting**
    - Duration: 35 min
    - What to do: Paint a subject in one hue to plan value.
    - What you learn: A value plan saves colour chaos.
    - Success looks like: Strong value structure in one colour.

18. **Apple Study**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Paint a single apple with highlight and core shadow.
    - What you learn: One object holds the full technique.
    - Success looks like: A round, lit, believable apple.

19. **Sky & Cloud Wash**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Paint a graded sky with soft cloud edges.
    - What you learn: Skies need gentle value.
    - Success looks like: Believable depth in the sky.

20. **Foliage Mass**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Suggest a tree using layered greens and fan texture.
    - What you learn: Mass before detail in nature.
    - Success looks like: Tree reads from a distance.

21. **Water Reflection**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Paint water with broken horizontal reflections.
    - What you learn: Water mirrors, then breaks.
    - Success looks like: Convincing shimmer and stillness.

22. **Simple Landscape**
    - Duration: 45 min
    - What to do: Compose a small landscape using the above.
    - What you learn: Elements combine into a scene.
    - Success looks like: A coherent little landscape.

23. **Still-Life in Colour**
    - Duration: 1 class
    - What to do: Paint a 3-object still life in full colour.
    - What you learn: Colour unifies a setup.
    - Success looks like: A harmonious, lit still life.

24. **Acrylic Final Piece**
    - Duration: 1–2 classes
    - What to do: Plan and finish one painting start to end.
    - What you learn: Process becomes habit.
    - Success looks like: A resolved acrylic painting.

## Outcome
You can mix any colour you see, build a painting in confident layers, and use the brush to describe texture, edge, and light.

## Parent & Student Notes
- Acrylic dries fast, which can feel like pressure at first — that's expected. A small misting spray and a stay-wet palette at home let her keep paint workable and practise layering between classes.
- Remind her that value (light and dark) matters more than the "right" colour. If a study looks flat, it's usually a value problem, not a hue problem.
- The single apple and the small landscape are the real tests of the phase — if those read as round and as a place, the techniques have landed.
- Keep a "happy accidents" pile. Acrylic is forgiving and layers over mistakes, so experimentation at home is encouraged, not feared.

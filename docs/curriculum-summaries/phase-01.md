# Phase 01 — Hand Control

## Overview
Phase 1, "Hand Control," is the foundation of the academy journey — five weeks spent building the most basic and most important skill of all: a steady, intentional line. Through 14 guided exercises, students learn to move the pencil with confidence rather than hesitation, and with 23 student examples already produced, newcomers can see exactly the level of control this phase develops.

## Focus Areas
- Pencil grip variations (tripod, overhand, underhand) and when each serves the line
- Line weight control through pressure and speed modulation
- Pressure sensitivity — from whisper-light hatching to grounded shadow lines

## Tools Introduced
- Graphite pencils HB, 2B, 4B, 6B — a range of softness so tone can be built gradually
- Kneaded eraser — lifts graphite without damaging the paper tooth
- A3 cartridge paper, 120–160 gsm — sturdy enough for repeated shading
- Pencil sharpener and sandpaper block — keeps a long, controllable lead
- Blending stumps — soften and feather tone without using fingers

## Exercises

1. **First Lines**
   - Duration: 10 min
   - What to do: Fill a page with continuous looping scribbles for two minutes without lifting the pencil.
   - What you learn: A loose wrist makes freer, more confident lines.
   - Success looks like: Scribbles look relaxed, not tight or hesitant.

2. **Tripod Grip Drill**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Practice drawing long lines using the tripod grip, pivoting from the shoulder.
   - What you learn: A stable grip gives control over long strokes.
   - Success looks like: Lines are smooth and reach across the page cleanly.

3. **Overhand Grip for Shading**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Hold the pencil overhand and shade broad areas with the side of the lead.
   - What you learn: The overhand grip covers tone quickly and evenly.
   - Success looks like: Tonal areas are even, with no visible scratch marks.

4. **Underhand Grip for Detail**
   - Duration: 10 min
   - What to do: Use the underhand grip near the tip for tight, precise marks.
   - What you learn: Hand position changes the kind of control you get.
   - Success looks like: Fine details sit exactly where you intend.

5. **Pressure Scale**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Draw a gradient strip from the lightest whisper to the darkest press.
   - What you learn: Pressure is your first and most basic shading tool.
   - Success looks like: A smooth, even gradient with no harsh jumps.

6. **Hatching Bands**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Fill bands with parallel hatching at consistent spacing and angle.
   - What you learn: Parallel lines build tone systematically.
   - Success looks like: Bands read as even grey steps.

7. **Cross-Hatching Grid**
   - Duration: 25 min
   - What to do: Layer cross-hatch to deepen tone and practice changing direction.
   - What you learn: Overlapping lines control darkness precisely.
   - Success looks like: Tones deepen cleanly wherever lines cross.

8. **Contour Drawing**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Draw an object's outline in one continuous line, eyes on the object.
   - What you learn: Looking at the subject beats looking at the page.
   - Success looks like: The outline follows the object's true contour.

9. **Blind Contour**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Repeat the contour exercise without peeking at the paper.
   - What you learn: Trusting observation over results loosens the hand.
   - Success looks like: The drawing feels connected to the object, not the page.

10. **Gesture Poses**
    - Duration: 20 min
    - What to do: Capture 30-second figure or object gestures focusing on movement.
    - What you learn: Gesture captures life before accuracy.
    - Success looks like: Poses read as movement, not stiff outlines.

11. **Ellipse Practice**
    - Duration: 20 min
    - What to do: Draw circles and ellipses at many angles and sizes.
    - What you learn: Ellipses are the basis of most forms.
    - Success looks like: Ellipses close cleanly without flat spots.

12. **Cylinder & Cube Forms**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Shade a cube and a cylinder using a single light source.
    - What you learn: Basic forms explain almost everything else.
    - Success looks like: Forms look 3D with a consistent light.

13. **Sphere Study**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Render a sphere with full tonal range and a cast shadow.
    - What you learn: The sphere teaches all tonal logic.
    - Success looks like: Smooth value roll-off from highlight to core shadow.

14. **Still-Life of Three Objects**
    - Duration: 1 class
    - What to do: Compose and draw three objects combining every skill above.
    - What you learn: Separate skills combine into a finished drawing.
    - Success looks like: A confident, well-toned small still life.

## Outcome
By the end of Phase 1 you can draw any form from observation with a steady, intentional line — and your hand no longer fights the pencil.

## Parent & Student Notes
- Practise a little every day rather than a lot once a week — even five minutes of loose scribbles or hatching keeps the wrist relaxed and the hand learning between classes.
- Expect the first few exercises to feel awkward; this is completely normal. Confidence arrives around exercise 10, not exercise 1, so be patient with the early tightness.
- Mastery is assessed by how freely the line moves and whether tone builds evenly — not by producing a "perfect" picture. We look for relaxed, intentional marks.
- Keep every page in a folder. The week-5 still life is best appreciated by flipping back through the early scribbles to see how far the hand has come.

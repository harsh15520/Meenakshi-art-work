# Phase 02 — Proportions

## Overview
Phase 2, "Proportions," runs over six weeks and teaches the student to see accurately before adding a single detail — how to measure, place, and balance a subject on the page. Across 18 exercises, students build the observation and composition habits that turn a drawing from "looks wrong" into "looks right," supported by 31 existing student examples.

## Focus Areas
- Measuring with sight-size and comparative proportion (thumb-on-pencil)
- Negative space and the envelope — blocking the whole before the parts
- Compositional balance — rule of thirds, weight, and breathing room

## Tools Introduced
- Graphite pencils (same HB–6B set from Phase 1)
- Printed reference photos at A4 — a stable subject to measure against
- Viewfinder / framing L's — find the crop before you draw
- Toned charcoal paper — mid-tone ground makes value obvious
- White charcoal pencil — recover highlights on toned ground

## Exercises

1. **Sight-Size Measuring**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Measure a bottle's height against its width using your pencil as a ruler.
   - What you learn: Comparison beats guesswork for accuracy.
   - Success looks like: Proportions match the object closely.

2. **Thumb Measuring**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Use thumb-on-pencil to check the relative sizes of parts.
   - What you learn: A quick gauge keeps relationships honest.
   - Success looks like: Parts relate correctly to one another.

3. **The Envelope**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Draw the outer bounding shape of a subject before any detail.
   - What you learn: The whole comes before the parts.
   - Success looks like: Everything fits inside the envelope.

4. **Negative Space Drawing**
   - Duration: 25 min
   - What to do: Draw the spaces around an object, not the object itself.
   - What you learn: Negative space reveals true shape.
   - Success looks like: The object appears correctly as a byproduct.

5. **Plumb Lines**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Drop vertical references to align features on a face.
   - What you learn: Verticals keep features from drifting.
   - Success looks like: Features align on true verticals.

6. **Angle Checking**
   - Duration: 20 min
   - What to do: Compare internal angles with your pencil as a protractor.
   - What you learn: Angles describe form better than outlines.
   - Success looks like: Slopes match the reference exactly.

7. **Rule of Thirds**
   - Duration: 15 min
   - What to do: Place a horizon and subject on third-lines in a sketch.
   - What you learn: Off-centre placement feels balanced.
   - Success looks like: The composition reads as intentional.

8. **Value Thumbnails**
   - Duration: 25 min
   - What to do: Make tiny 4-value sketches of a scene before drawing.
   - What you learn: Planning value saves rework later.
   - Success looks like: Thumbnails clearly guide the final.

9. **Eye-Level & Perspective**
   - Duration: 25 min
   - What to do: Sketch a box from eye level, noting the vanishing cues.
   - What you learn: Viewpoint changes everything.
   - Success looks like: Box reads as grounded in space.

10. **One-Point Interior**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Draw a room using a single vanishing point.
    - What you learn: Interiors follow simple, repeatable rules.
    - Success looks like: Walls and floor converge correctly.

11. **Two-Point Cube**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Construct a cube with two vanishing points.
    - What you learn: Two points describe real objects.
    - Success looks like: Cube sits convincingly in perspective.

12. **Portrait Block-In**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Map a face using thirds and the envelope.
    - What you learn: Faces obey proportion too.
    - Success looks like: Features sit in correct relationships.

13. **Hand Construction**
    - Duration: 35 min
    - What to do: Draw a hand using simple masses, not outlines.
    - What you learn: Hands are built, not traced.
    - Success looks like: Hand reads as volumes, not a glove.

14. **Foot & Simple Anatomy**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Study foot structure with basic forms.
    - What you learn: Understanding bones improves the drawing.
    - Success looks like: Foot looks structured, not flat.

15. **Foreshortening**
    - Duration: 30 min
    - What to do: Draw an arm or leg coming toward you.
    - What you learn: Foreshortening compresses apparent length.
    - Success looks like: Limb reads as advancing in space.

16. **Composition with Weight**
    - Duration: 25 min
    - What to do: Rearrange a still life for visual balance.
    - What you learn: Balance is felt, then adjusted.
    - Success looks like: Layout feels stable and calm.

17. **Cropping & Framing**
    - Duration: 20 min
    - What to do: Use L-viewfinders to find the strongest crop.
    - What you learn: The crop decides the story.
    - Success looks like: The chosen crop strengthens the subject.

18. **Long Observational Study**
    - Duration: 1 class
    - What to do: Spend a full class on one accurate drawing.
    - What you learn: Patience produces accuracy.
    - Success looks like: A resolved, proportionally true study.

## Outcome
You can place anything accurately on the page — a face, a hand, a room — and arrange it so the composition feels balanced before you ever add detail.

## Parent & Student Notes
- Accuracy is a skill of the eye, not the hand — encourage your daughter to measure out loud ("this is twice as tall as that") while practising at home; saying it reinforces seeing it.
- The biggest breakthrough is noticing her own proportion mistake before the teacher does. Celebrate those moments; they mean the eye is now leading the hand.
- Don't rush the long observational study at the end. A full class on one drawing is where everything from the phase comes together — slowness here is the point.
- Keep the printed references and viewfinders handy at home; a few minutes of "envelope then negative space" sketching of everyday objects builds the habit between lessons.

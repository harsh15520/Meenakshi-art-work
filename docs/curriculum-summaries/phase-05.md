# Phase 05 — Signature Style

## Overview
Phase 5, "Signature Style," is the open-ended final stage — ten weeks and beyond — where technique becomes a personal voice. Through 8 guided exercises and 19 student examples, students develop their own subject and palette, work in series to find consistency, and learn to present and sequence a finished body of work.

## Focus Areas
- Developing a personal subject and palette
- Series work — repeating a motif to find your voice
- Presenting and sequencing a body of work (portfolio)

## Tools Introduced
- Your chosen medium at full strength — oils or acrylics, used confidently
- Portfolio case and a digital documentation setup — photograph every work
- A variety of supports — canvas, board, and paper for different ideas
- Framing and presentation materials — the work deserves a finished edge

## Exercises

1. **Choosing Your Subject**
   - Duration: 1 class
   - What to do: List the motifs that genuinely pull you and pick one.
   - What you learn: Voice starts with appetite.
   - Success looks like: A clear personal direction.

2. **Your Palette Statement**
   - Duration: 1 class
   - What to do: Develop a signature limited palette from your favourites.
   - What you learn: Palette is identity.
   - Success looks like: A palette that feels like you.

3. **Series Planning**
   - Duration: 1 class
   - What to do: Plan five works around one motif and its variations.
   - What you learn: A series reveals a voice.
   - Success looks like: A coherent series brief.

4. **First Series Painting**
   - Duration: 1–2 classes
   - What to do: Execute the first piece of your series.
   - What you learn: Theory becomes practice.
   - Success looks like: A strong opening work.

5. **Variation Studies**
   - Duration: 1–2 classes
   - What to do: Repaint the motif with deliberate changes.
   - What you learn: Variation deepens intent.
   - Success looks like: Clear progression across the studies.

6. **Personal Master Copy**
   - Duration: 1–2 classes
   - What to do: Study and reinterpret an artist you admire.
   - What you learn: Influence is a teacher.
   - Success looks like: A respectful, personal copy.

7. **Portfolio Sequencing**
   - Duration: 1 class
   - What to do: Arrange your works for narrative flow.
   - What you learn: Order shapes meaning.
   - Success looks like: A sequenced, story-driven set.

8. **Portfolio Presentation**
   - Duration: 1–2 classes
   - What to do: Photograph, frame, and present your body of work.
   - What you learn: Presentation completes the work.
   - Success looks like: A polished, presentable portfolio.

## Outcome
You leave with a cohesive body of personal work and the confidence to keep developing your own visual language.

## Parent & Student Notes
- There is no single "right" outcome here — style reveals itself through repetition, not a sudden decision. Encourage her to keep painting the same motif even after it feels familiar; that's where the voice appears.
- Document everything: a simple phone photo of each piece in good light, stored in a folder, becomes the portfolio later. Consistent documentation from day one saves stress at presentation time.
- Studying an admired artist is allowed and encouraged — influence is a teacher, not copying. The goal is a respectful, personal reinterpretation, not a replica.
- This phase is self-paced (10+ weeks) by design. Let her linger on a piece she loves; the confidence to keep developing her own language matters more than finishing quickly.
